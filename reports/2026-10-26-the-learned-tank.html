The periscope

The net never sees the world's lists. It feels a compass of eight sectors round its hull and reads four exact contacts, as a submarine reads its plot. Drag the contacts round the tank. Ahead is up.

Calling friends

A team's learned tanks share one queue of requests. A call lives 10 s, the queue keeps 16, and each tank calls at most every 2 s. Every call is also a Ping in the world, so scripted teammates answer it too, and their pings come back into the queue: one language for both brains.

Signal headhold, help (come to me), attack (that enemy), take (that depot)
→

Training: generations on the CPU

Evolution strategies: each generation tries 48 random nudges of all 15,791 weights, forward and back, on the same 12 training grounds (1,164 one-minute episodes), ranks them, and steps toward the nudges that scored better. One generation takes about a quarter of a second on an M1's eight cores.

phase 1, periscope sensesphase 1, compass only (from gen 200)phase 3, drive and shoot

Score of the net itself on its generation's 12 grounds. Phase 1 pays up to 2.5 a ground (the way closed, a reach, the sooner); phase 3 pays 3.2 for three direct hits and a kill. The periscope net passed every ground by generation 67.

Then the census

The same census that judges every weight change seats a team of five nets against the scripted defaults, 110 seeds, 11 maps, three matches a seed. Zero is the scripted brain.

The trained team reads 3.17 ± 0.45 below a random team. On the grounds nothing shot back and every pillbox was neutral; in a match the nearest pillbox is often hostile and the nearest enemy is a team of five under its own guns. It drives straight at them. The census did its job: it says by how much this is not yet a better tank, and points at what to teach next.
