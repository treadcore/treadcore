                       FORTIFY: A CASTLE'S GATE

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           15 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 11b: a goal under which the squad builds to a plan instead of
    a tile at a time. The castle is three pillboxes that cover each
    other (two towers flanking a gate, one behind the point), a curtain
    wall across the threat's side, and a road out through the gate.
    Alone on the generated island for five minutes the squad built
    all three pillboxes, the whole wall and four of the five road
    tiles, and the way in from the front runs through the gate: 12.0
    tiles open, 19.9 with the gate shut. The first gate was three
    tiles wide and turned nobody.

INDEX TERMS

    artificial intelligence, game design, path finding.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Paul's notes asked for walls and roads placed to protect pillboxes,
    and castle-like entrances: choke points under guard towers.

II. BACKGROUND

    The Defender (step 6d) already rings its home depot with mines and
    walls, but around itself only, gated toward the centre, and it
    remembers tiles rather than following a plan. A Brain has no map;
    the world's answers (Built, PillboxBuilt, Rejected with why,
    PillboysAbandoned with why) are how it learns the ground.

III. METHOD

    fortify.rs draws the castle as a list of squad orders: the towers
    TOWER_R (4) out at 32 degrees either side of the threat, 4.2 tiles
    apart; the rear pillbox REAR_R (3) behind, 6.7 from each; the wall
    at WALL_R (5) over 70 degrees either side, its ends first; the road
    from 3 to 7 tiles out. The Commander feeds one order at a time to
    an idle squad, fells forest when the material is short (and first,
    when a planned tile is forest: that pays for the rest), and guards
    the point meanwhile.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. The plan on paper, tested: every pillbox inside the others'
       reach and outside their spacing, the towers on either side of
       the gate, no wall behind the point.
    2. On the island: built in five minutes, but FAIL on the route
       test: the route was one waypoint. The router keeps waypoints
       and draws straight legs between them, so the test measures the
       legs now.
    3. FAIL: with the gate shut the way in was 12.8 tiles against
       12.0. Drawn as text: the gate was three tiles wide (13 degrees
       either side caught the tiles beside the axis), and the test
       shut one. GATE_HALF is 8 degrees: a one- or two-tile gate. The
       ring need not join side to side: the router takes no diagonal
       step past a wall.
    4. The wider wall cost the rear pillbox its time. The guns go
       first now: a wall nobody covers only slows an attacker. PASS:
       3 of 3, 10 of 10, 4 of 5; 12.0 through the gate, 19.9 shut.

V. RESULTS

    make test: sim 36, brain 19, client 21, net 14, proto 3.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The gate is a single tile on the threat's bearing. That is the
    choke point, and it is also the depot's own front door: a side that
    builds it drives out single file, under its own towers.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Concrete for the wall, twice the material.
    2. Whether a RuleBot attacker, which routes around hostile pillbox
       rings, goes round the wall's end instead of through the towers.
       The proof here is the plain router's.
    3. The fleet ally and pings (11c, 11d).

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. 11c, the coordination interface.
