ABSTRACT

    The second session ran ROADMAP step 0, "prove what exists", on the
    code written in the first. The compiler accepted every crate first
    time, including the Bevy client; the dev-profile engine finished
    building as a side effect of `cargo test --workspace` in 4 min 28 s
    on the VM, now 8 GB. The tests then found three bugs the compiler
    could not: a UDP receive buffer too small for the Welcome packet
    (no client could ever join), a straight-line Move that stopped at
    the river (a bot parked on the bank for a whole match), and the
    Makefile's SECONDS colliding with bash's own counter. The first
    traced match was a twenty-second melee with no indirect fire; the
    rule bot's doctrine was rewritten in four passes until a trace
    showed the design: 15 shells, 13 of them spotted. Mid-session the
    brief grew to sixteen players, mostly defenders, with alliances
    and betrayal; sixteen seats were built the same evening and
    sixteen bots fight a traced match in under a second. A requirement
    that the Pillboys follow the prior art's builder was checked and written
    into the design. The organization's rules (how to join, propose,
    review, leave, be recognised) and the release cycle were written
    down before a second contributor exists. The window does not open
    in this VM: it has no Vulkan driver.

I. INTRODUCTION

    Journal 0001 ended with eight open problems, the first of which was
    that nothing above the simulation had been through the compiler.
    ROADMAP step 0 says nothing else starts until that is closed. This
    session set out to close it: build everything, run every test, run
    the traced match and read it, run the four-peer lobby, open the
    window; fix what those find; write down what was learned.

    Paul's direction arrived in four messages during the session, each
    enlarging it: read everything and recommend (the start); write the
    roadmap's backlog, define the implementation and release cycle,
    and set up the organization with its processes; take a pasted
    audit of a prior-art tutorial as the requirement for the Pillboys and
    report on it; and change the game to sixteen players, most of them
    base-defence brains, with alliances, friendly fire off inside
    them, and betrayals under a three-way balance. The session did the
    first three in full and the mechanical part of the fourth; the
    rest of the fourth is written into CONCEPT.md and the roadmap as
    steps with proofs.

VI. DISCUSSION

    A. The compiler was not the problem

    0001 predicted "a round of small fixes" when the upper crates met
    the compiler. There were none: every crate compiled first time,
    the client included. The three bugs were all in the gap between
    "compiles" and "works", and each was found by exactly the kind of
    proof the roadmap asked for: a unit test over loopback, a trace
    read by a person, a trace header read by a person. The lesson is
    the roadmap's own: do not start the next step until the proof of
    this one has been run, because the proofs find different things
    from the compiler.

    B. A truncated datagram is silent

    `recv_from` into a small buffer does not fail; it returns the first
    N bytes and throws the rest away, and bincode then fails on a
    length it cannot satisfy, which the transport logs at trace and
    counts as a dropped packet. Nothing above it ever saw a Welcome.
    The buffer is now the UDP maximum. The general rule: a receive
    buffer is sized to the protocol's largest message, never to a
    "reasonable" number.

    C. One router

    The simulation moved in straight lines and left routing to the
    caller, and neither caller could do it: the client pushed one
    waypoint and forgot the rest, and a Brain has no map at all. The
    fix was to move the routing down into the world, where the map is,
    and it made both callers simpler. It also exposed a second bug the
    first was hiding (the turn radius at a bridge), which is the usual
    shape of a fix at the right layer.

    D. A doctrine is tuned by reading

    Four passes over the rule bot, each a one-trace diagnosis: they all
    drive to the centre; they all advance at once; the recon is over
    the tank; the test counts four bots on a sixteen-start map. The
    per-second state lines in the trace (position, bay, link
    percentages) did the diagnosing; the recon link reading 91 % was
    the whole clue in pass 2. The trace format earned its keep today.

    E. Sixteen cost an evening

    Because the world was plain data and the map a function of a seed,
    sixteen seats were a constant, sixteen points, a ring road, a
    naming function and two tests. The cost of the day-one decision
    to keep the sim plain is being repaid.

    F. Rules before people

    The governance and release documents are written for an
    organization of one. That is deliberate: the processes are
    cheapest to write when nobody is waiting on them and no case is
    pending, and the second contributor should find them, not
    negotiate them.
