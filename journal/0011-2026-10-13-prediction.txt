                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org

ABSTRACT

    Step 4 gives a client its own tank to run: a partial copy of the
    world built from the Welcome, hull orders applied the moment they
    are sent, one tick at the tick rate by the function the host runs,
    and every snapshot adopted, re-applied over, and re-driven. The
    check is bit for bit whenever nothing is in flight. There is no
    tc and no root in this VM, so the transport grew a lag queue that
    stands in for netem. With 100 ms each way the predicted tank moves
    250 ms before the snapshot shows it and matches the host in 53 of
    53 checks; the lagged lobby test's three seats match in 1 602 of
    1 602. The first lagged run found fourteen mismatches at zero
    tiles off: firing turns the hull, and the copy did not fire. 37
    tests.

INDEX TERMS

    prediction, reconciliation, acked seq, drive_tank, lag queue,
    tick_state, lobby-test.


I. INTRODUCTION

    The roadmap's remaining step that one machine can prove. A client
    had been drawing the last snapshot interpolated by wall time, which
    hides a 100 ms link and not a 400 ms one, and its own orders took
    a round trip to show. The question was whether the client can run
    its own tank without ever disagreeing with the host, and what it
    takes to know when it does.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0010: the window unproven; step 4 next. Step 2's hash
    was written with this step in mind ("a client that also runs the
    world compares"), but a client is shown what it may see and not
    the world, so a whole-world hash on the client was never going to
    be possible. The roadmap's proof clause asked for `tc netem delay
    200ms`; the VM has neither tc nor root.

III. METHOD

    The sim first: `step_tanks`'s body became a free function,
    `drive_tank`, and the 6d replay was run before anything else
    touched it (60 of 60). Then `World::predict`: one player's hull
    orders applied and that tank driven, nothing else stepped. Then
    the Predictor in treadcore-net, where it can be tested without an
    engine; then the host's `acked` on every snapshot (wire version
    10); then the window.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. `drive_tank` out of `step_tanks`; replay of the 6d recording:
        60 of 60. `World::predict`; `Tank::tick_state`; the sim test
        `prediction_drives_the_tank_as_the_host_does`: a copy with one
        tank, the same Move at the same tick, equal bits all the way
        across the river.
     2. `Snapshot { acked }`, version 10. The Predictor: order, tick,
        reconcile; history of 64 ticks; at most 40 ticks ahead. The
        Client builds one from the Welcome, feeds it every order,
        snapshot and event; `predict_tick` for the owner of the loop.
     3. `Socket::set_lag` and `TREADCORE_LAG_MS`. The net test with
        100 ms each way: first run failed its own assertion, which
        asked the predicted tick to lead the snapshot's at the moment
        a snapshot lands, when the two are by construction equal. The
        lead that matters is wall time: the predicted tank moved
        251 ms before the host's snapshot showed it moving. Assertion
        rewritten; 53 checks, 0 mismatches.
     4. The headless seat predicts at the tick rate and logs a line;
        lobby-test takes LAG and reads the lines. First lagged run:
        PASS on every old clause, 14 mismatches in the new one, all
        but one at 0.000 tiles off, every one at a tick the seat
        fired. Firing sets the hull heading to the target, and the
        copy only took Move and Stop. `Command::predicted` now names
        the four hull orders; the copy drops the shell it spawns.
     5. Second lagged run: 598 snapshots a seat, 540, 552 and 510
        checks, 0 mismatches. The plain run: 0 of 0 mismatches too,
        PASS. The window: predicted tank drawn in place of the
        snapshot's, interpolated from its own last tick; NET panel
        line; release build in 23 s with the three old warnings.
     6. 37 tests; documents; this entry.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The step's only finding was the hull turning to fire, and it was
    found by the check, not by watching: a standing tank, zero tiles
    off, state unequal. A distance tolerance would have passed it and
    the next Move would have driven off a wrong heading for a tick or
    two, a correction too small to see and too frequent to be nothing.
    The bit-for-bit comparison is the whole value of making predict
    call drive_tank: the two cannot differ by rounding, so any
    difference is a rule the copy does not run, and the log names the
    tick.

    What the copy does not run, by design: mines it does not know, the
    swarm, damage. Each would show as a mismatch with a distance, and
    none did in the lobby runs, because a RuleBot tank seldom drives
    onto an unknown mine in a minute.

    The roadmap's "driving feels immediate" is 250 ms of lead on a
    200 ms round trip, which is the round trip plus the time to the
    next snapshot: the tank moves when the key goes down. Whether that
    feels right is the playtest's to say.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The window: still unproven in this VM.
    2. The predicted tank is corrected, never smoothed: a mismatch
       snaps. On a straight road there is none to snap over.
    3. Only the own tank. Allies' tanks, drones and shells are the
       snapshot interpolated by wall time, as before.
    4. Rejoin (steps 7, 8) and the pillbox view are where they were.
