ABSTRACT

    The window opened in this VM for the first time, a day after the
    headless game was proven. The Vulkan driver was one package; the
    package brought a segfault in LLVM on every Bevy binary, which was
    musl's 128 KiB thread stack under lavapipe's shader compiler, and
    the fix is a twelve-line preload the build now makes. A solo match
    and three multiclient matches through the introducer were played
    by script and photographed: nine frames, a lab report with the
    multiclient explained end to end, a gallery on the front page.
    The HUD's rtt turned out to be heartbeat age and was fixed; five
    more findings were recorded and not fixed. An interface study
    from the prior art's screen and two other kinds of tank interface
    ends with a layout. 40 tests; 20 Hz held on the CPU rasteriser.

INDEX TERMS

    mesa-vulkan-swrast, lavapipe, musl, pthread_setattr_default_np,
    bigstack.so, Xwayland, xdotool, grim, introducer, rejoin, rtt,

I. INTRODUCTION

    Every journal since 0002 has carried "the window: unproven in this
    VM" as an open problem. 0012 closed step 7 on one machine and left
    the window where it was. The instruction for this session was to
    take pictures, which meant the window had to open, and to write
    up the first playtest build with the multiclient explained and a
    gallery that sells the game. Then, mid-session, to look at the
    interfaces of other tank games and say how treadcore's should be
    laid out.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0012: the window unproven; the predicted tank
    corrected, never smoothed; rejoin and the pillbox view; the
    two-router proof. BUILD.md had named the fix for the window since
    12 October: `mesa-vulkan-swrast`, needing root. copal, the install
    that made this VM, had Mesa's GL drivers in its full monty and no
    Vulkan driver.

    by hand here. Then the window, and whatever it did. Then a scripted

    then the multiclient: the step 7 arrangement, an introducer and no
    broadcast, with the window as one of three peers, and a kill and

     2. The window: adapter found (llvmpipe, Vulkan), window created,
        segfault. The engine-only spinner: segfault. Under Xwayland:
        one frame presented, segfault. gdb: thread 7, lavapipe's,
        inside llvm::AsmPrinter::doFinalization via MCJIT. musl 1.2.6
        gives threads 128 KiB. LP_NUM_THREADS 0 and 1: segfault.
     3. bigstack.c: a constructor, pthread_setattr_default_np, 8 MiB.
        LD_PRELOAD, the spinner ran for its timeout. Into
        scripts/, configure.sh (also reports the ICDs found), run.sh,
        config.env's PRELOAD.
     4. First shoot, fullscreen: the focus command missed the Xwayland
        window and the frames were of the terminal. False start.
        Focused by window address and checked, then nine frames of a
        solo match: HUD, bubble, links, recon, a refused shot inside
        minimum range, the tank dead at tick 1 615.
     5. Second solo shoot with Tab first and a valid range: the
        bubble with three interceptors and a recon, a spotted round in
        flight, the squad refused "no forest there" one tile off, the
        tank under fire from the swarm and a pillbox. Two frames lost
        to a wheel click that switched workspace.
     6. The multiclient: introducer on 47300, host with identity 1,
        a lagged headless seat, the lagged window. Elected: the host.
        Welcomed as player 0 at tick 1. Killed at sixty seconds, back
        at tick 1 294: "seat 0 'alice' is back ... sequences given
        back". But the frames: the rtt read 200 to 450 ms on a 60 ms
        link, and several frames were of the desktop, the window
        tiled small under the terminal Paul was typing in.
     7. The rtt: the client echoed the host tick from the last

     8. Nine frames chosen and quantised to 256 colours (49 to 70 KB),

VI. DISCUSSION

    The segfault is the session's lesson, and it is a libc lesson,
    not a graphics one. Nothing in the backtrace was ours; nothing in
    Mesa was wrong; LLVM did what it does on the stack it was given,
    and musl gave what it gives. glibc's 8 MiB default hides this
    class of fault from everyone who develops on it, which is nearly
    everyone, and an Alpine VM with no GPU is where it surfaces. The
    fix belongs to the build and not to the code, because the code
    did nothing wrong, and configure is where a machine's quirks are
    found and answered.

    The rtt finding is a reminder that a number on a HUD is only as
    good as the freshest input to it. The formula was right since
    0009; the operand was stale. The bit-for-bit prediction check
    beside it, zero mismatches across 784 checks under lag, is the
    better number, and it had nothing to hide.

    The playtest was scripted, which is why it was repeatable and why
    it is not yet a playtest. A script fires at the same point every
    time and aims the squad one tile off every time; it does not feel
    the 250 ms of lead, and it does not get frightened. The first
    thing a human will meet is finding 7: thirty seconds, standing,
    dead. The bots are doing what the Defender and RuleBot journals
    say they do; the newcomer has no first minute. That goes to the
    interface study, which puts the first minute's six lines on the
    lobby screen, and to the design, which may want a grace.

    On the references: Bolo's screen is the one to learn from because
    its problem was ours. Its lamp grids and its newswire are what
    a player reads at a glance in a fight; the stock HUD graphics are
    what a designer draws when nobody will read them. The garage
    screen's lesson is only that a match has a before and an after.
