                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org

ABSTRACT

    Step 6b makes every change of sides in play a proposal: to join,
    to recruit, to swap teams, or to merge whole teams, put by one
    player to another, answered by the one asked or anyone on their
    team within thirty seconds, and resolved by the world under the
    three-way rule: no outcome may leave fewer than three sides with a
    living tank, or give one side more than half the living tanks. The
    rule is checked when a proposal is made and again when accepted,
    and every resolution (accepted, declined, refused with the reason,
    lapsed, overtaken) is an event to everyone, so a betrayal is public
    the moment it happens. Step 6's alliance commands became sugar
    over this. The proof is a recorded match: two sides of eight refuse
    every proposal; of three sides a defection is carried out and the
    defector's recon spots for its new side; recruiting past half is
    refused; a swap is allowed at any size. 31 tests pass.

INDEX TERMS

I. INTRODUCTION

    The brief wanted betrayal: a mid-match proposal to swap sides or
    recruit, "but only in a three way balance type way." Step 6 built
    sides and the prior art's way of forming them; this step builds the way of
    changing them, and the rule that keeps a change from being the end
    of the game.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The rule does what the brief asked, and the test says where the
    edges are: two sides can never change, a side may grow to half and
    no further, a swap is always open. Whether half is the right line,
    and whether the sides should be counted by living tanks or by
    seats, is a balance question for play; both are one constant.

    The slicing accident is recorded because the journal records false
    starts. Nothing was lost (the last commit held the code), and the
    lesson is a working rule: replace a function by its own bounds,
    never from one landmark to the next.
