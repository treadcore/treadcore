    THE DEFENDER AND THE STORMING ATTACKER: ROADMAP STEP 6D

                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 6d replaces the Fortifier with the Defender, the brief's
    defensive doctrine with eyes: a recon on the approach that moves
    to the depot of whoever shot it last, a sidestep under fire, the
    moat first, then a pillbox at the mouth of the walls, a road, mines
    across the approach, the walls; and the first bots that propose
    and answer alliances. The attacker got the other half: a recon
    kept out of the enemy's bubble, a squad recalled under fire, and a
    storm when shelling a repaired tank goes nowhere. The proof ran
    ten seeds of sixteen bots, four attacking: every match to the time
    limit, attackers alive 2 of 40 against defenders 84 of 120, the
    defenders keeping 101 of 120 depots. The roadmap's second clause,
    most kills by pillbox, did not come true: 65 kills by shell to 1,
    because the gun out-ranges the pillbox 60 tiles to 8. Five
    iterations of trace, finding, fix are the chronology. 35 tests.

INDEX TERMS

    defender, attacker, storm, counter-battery, moat, mouth pillbox,
    alliance, contested depot, proof.


I. INTRODUCTION

    The roadmap's last step on the prior art track: a brain that uses
    everything 3, 6c, 6 and 6b built. The brief wanted a defensive
    game; the question was whether a bot playing it holds a depot
    against bots that do not, and what kills whom.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0009: no bot proposes or answers; pillboxes fire 0 to
    2 times a match (backlog 14); concealment unexercised; the window
    unproven. The Fortifier of 6c: post, two interceptors, a gun with
    seven tiles of sight, a moat and a wall ring. Twelve of them
    against four rule bots: 2.4 kills a match, nobody storms, no
    pillbox fires. The generated map has no forest cover within reach
    of any depot (checked: 0 of 17), so hiding in trees is not a
    defender's tactic here. Every spoke has a neutral pillbox ten
    tiles from the depot toward the centre, on the approach.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. The Defender written over the Fortifier: capture, repair, the
        mouth pillbox, road, approach mines, moat, walls; recon at 16
        tiles toward the centre; proposals. First run: twelve build
        11 pillboxes, 207 mines, 138 walls; 8 of 24 proposals
        accepted; 0.6 kills a match. The attackers do nothing.
     2. Trace: attacker seat 0 sits at its start for 300 s with no
        recon, both shot down in eight seconds over the defended
        depot by the defender's bubble (a recon inside the bubble is
        an intruder, by 0002's design). Blind, it never advances.
        Fix: the recon orbits ten tiles short of what it watches;
        blind counts as low, so the bot goes home for one.
     3. Run: 8.4 kills a match, 7.6 alive. Trace: three defenders die
        in the first twenty seconds, to the opening salvo from the
        side, while their recons watch the centre; then the
        survivors' counter-battery kills every attacker by 130 s.
        Fix: the recon watches the depot of whoever shot last (the
        depot owner is public: deduction, not cheating), moved by
        Retask; a hit tank sidesteps for four seconds.
     4. Run: 6.0 kills, 10 alive, no storms. Trace: now the attackers
        die first, at their starts, holding still for a squad sent to
        capture the spoke pillbox in the first second; the storm
        never triggers because contact is never continuous. Fix: a
        hit tank recalls its squad and moves; the storm clock
        accumulates contact. Also one answer a think: two answers in
        one tick made "no such proposal" eleven times a match.
     5. Run: 7.5 kills, 8.5 alive; the proof's first clause holds and
        its second does not. Then `fortification_pays` failed: the
        fortified defender died sooner than the bare one, 125 k
        against 134 k ticks. Per-seed diagnostics and an event dump
        of seed 8: the scripted attacker walked through the half-
        built ring onto the depot, a contested depot repairs nobody,
        and the two tanks traded direct fire to a mutual kill at
        tick 3942. The ring was half built because the pillbox, road
        and approach came first. Fix: the moat first. The proof
        widened past where it was: 144 000 against 134 206.
     6. `defenders_hold` written with the roadmap's clauses, the
        second as a printed split rather than an assertion that
        would fail. 35 tests; a traced five-minute defended match
        replays (60 of 60); lobby-test; documents; this entry.

    alive 2 of 40, defenders 84 of 120; depots at the end, defenders

    1 pillbox, 1 strike; 2 pillbox shots; 62 pillboxes built.

    Tests: 35 pass (sim 23, proto 3, net 5, brain 4).

VI. DISCUSSION

    The step did what it set out to, and one of its predictions was
    wrong in a way worth keeping. Pillboxes do not kill in this game
    because a 60-tile gun with eyes settles matters before anyone is
    within 8 tiles of one; what a pillbox does is make a depot
    untakeable by a tank, and 101 of 120 depots held says it does.
    The roadmap now says so beside its original clause.

    Each round's finding was a bot doing the obvious thing in the
    wrong circumstance: a recon flown into a bubble, a tank standing
    still for its squad under fire, a wall ring built after the
    ornaments. None was a rule of the world; all were doctrine, and a
    trace found each in minutes. The match report from 0009 earned
    its place again: the kill split by cause is the whole story of
    this step in one line.

    The contested-depot rule (two tanks there, nothing happens) made
    the 6c failure: it turns a defender's home into a fair duel the
    moment an attacker stands on it. That is a good rule; it is what
    makes storming worth anything. The moat is the answer to it.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Storms are rare: the attacker that lives to twice its patience
       is rare. A pillbox kill is rarer. A playtest decides whether
       that is the game or the bots.
    2. No bot uses cover; the generated map offers none at a depot.
    3. The window: unproven, and no keys for Retask, proposals or
       answers.
    4. Rejoin (steps 7, 8); the pillbox view (the first window).

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. Step 4, client-side prediction of the player's own tank.
    2. Steps 7 and 8 when a second machine is on the table.
