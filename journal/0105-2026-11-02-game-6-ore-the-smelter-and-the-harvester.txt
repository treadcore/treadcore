## Abstract

The plan's second stage of the economy is built. A map holds deposits
of ore, each a finite stock; on such a map a depot makes no shells and
a refinery gives fuel alone, so every shell was once ore a squad mined
and a smelter made. The smelter and the harvester stand beside their
resource on open ground; a harvest near a harvester yields six and the
tree returns in a minute. The scripted brain runs the supply: the base's
guard builds the smelter and fetches ore whenever the depot is short,
every other tank once after a fill-up, and no tank waits at a dry depot.
On the naval map for ten minutes the fleet's own bots built five
smelters, mined forty ore and smelted thirty-six shells. GAME_VERSION
is 6; the simulator's 64 tests and the brain's 153 pass. An old fault
came out under the new rule: the hunter lobbed one blind shell at a
neutral pillbox the squad was taking, which the depot's free shell had
hidden; a live target keeps the gun now.

## Session

Paul: "continue. implement the economy. commit and push."

The simulator first: Deposit on the world and the observation, the
deposit line in a map file and seeded deposits for maps that place
none, the scenario's ore flag (on unless said), the depot's rule, the
mine, the unload, the smelt, the two works at their resource, the
harvester's yield. Four tests, one of which found its own bug (the
test read the wrong deposit: the world had seeded eight of its own).

Then the brain: the ore run as a small state machine with a building
stage, two weights inside RANGES, the dry-depot rule. The end-to-end
test on the naval map found two things in turn: with the run gated on
a fill-up alone, no tank ever filled up in ten quiet minutes, so no
smelter was built; and before that, with the run free, the commander
in the neutral-pillbox scenario went off to build a smelter. The rule
that holds: the guard runs ore whenever the depot is short, the rest
once a fill-up.

Then the census line, the window, the chair's refusals, the records,
the book's chapter 13, and orrery's reading of ore and smelted.
