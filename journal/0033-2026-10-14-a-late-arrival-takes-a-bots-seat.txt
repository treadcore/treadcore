                     A LATE ARRIVAL TAKES A BOT'S SEAT

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           14 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 8 said a player who arrives while a match runs is refused
    until the next one. Now a peer that finds a running host through
    the introducer, while a bot's seat with a live tank is free, is
    seated in it mid-match under its own name. The world is told by
    order, Command::TakeSeat, so the replay and the world hash carry
    it: LISTEN=near LATE=1 make lobby-test has the server host, a fifth
    peer take bot seat 6 at tick 366, and the server's replay match
    every checkpoint.

INDEX TERMS

    networking, determinism, testing.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Step 8's two items left: a late arrival, and one server for
    several lobbies. The first is the one a person meets.

II. BACKGROUND

    The world hash is the whole world, serialised. A seat changing
    hands outside the orders would make every replay of such a match
    disagree with its own checkpoints. Bots run outside the sim, in the
    server and in the window.

III. METHOD

    The seat changes by order: Command::TakeSeat { name }, injected by
    the host into the tick's inbox for the bot's player; the world
    renames the seat, marks it a person's and tells everyone
    (Event::SeatTaken); a person's seat cannot be taken (LOOSE NUT).
    The host gives the lowest-numbered bot seat whose tank is alive and
    no peer holds (Host::free_seat). treadcore_brain::retire_taken
    drops the bot on the event, in the server's host loop and the
    window's.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. The sim order and event, and a sim test inside the alliance
       test's world: taken, renamed, refused the second time.
    2. The host: a pre-pass gives an unknown peer's Join a seat and
       queues the order; Seat::new factored out of Host::new. The net
       test a_late_arrival_takes_a_bots_seat: two late clients get
       seats 1 and 2, a third is refused.
    3. LATE=1 make lobby-test: FAIL. peer5 was told by the introducer
       that peer4 was hosting, and hosted a match of its own. A
       lobby joins a host only when the host answers its Ping, and a
       host answered a Ping only from a seated peer. Now it answers an
       unseated peer while a seat is free (and, through an introducer,
       pings toward it, as it does for a seat that dropped); with none
       free it stays silent and a late group elects among itself.
    4. FAIL again, on the test's own word: it wanted seat 4 or above.
       A headless host seats the three remote peers 0 to 2 and its own
       seat is a bot's: 3 was right. PASS.
    5. LATE=1, REJOIN=1, the plain test: PASS. LISTEN=near LATE=1:
       the test assumed the host was a peer and four seats introduced
       to the server; both counted the late one wrong. PASS: the
       server hosted, peer5 took seat 6, the replay matched.

V. RESULTS

    make test: sim 36, brain 10, client 21, net 14, proto 3.
    lobby-test: plain, REJOIN=1, LATE=1, LISTEN=near LATE=1: PASS.

VI. DISCUSSION

    Doing it by order cost one Command variant and made the replay
    proof free. A host-side change to the world would have been fewer
    lines and a replay that lies.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Whether any of it is fun (from 0027).
    2. One server for several lobbies (step 8's last).
    3. A late arrival over the LAN beacon alone: the host runs no
       lobby and does not shout, so only the introducer finds it.

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. A playtest over the LAN, late arrival included.
    2. One server for several lobbies.
