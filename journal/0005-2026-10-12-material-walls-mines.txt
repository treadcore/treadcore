ABSTRACT

    Step 6c gives the squad things to build. The squad fells a forest
    tile for four material and builds walls (impassable, block a flat
    shot, a splash knocks them to rubble), roads (bridges over water,
    built from the bank) and mines, the prior art's: hidden from all but the
    layer until found within a tile and a half, set off by a tank or
    squad on the tile, a shell's splash or an orthogonal neighbour, 50
    to the tank on it and 20 around, a squad in the blast lost, the
    tile cratered, and ignored by drones. Pillboxes hold eight shells
    for their owner's tank. A terrain-change event keeps clients'
    maps current. A minimal defender brain, the Fortifier, harvests,
    rings its home depot with mines and walls, and sits. The proof:
    over twenty seeds, against a scripted attacker that drives for the
    depot after 150 seconds, the fortified defender lived to tick
    137 664 in total against 94 885 bare (45 percent longer), survived
    18 matches to 5, and killed the attacker 20 times to 12, having
    built 431 things to none. The first run of that proof found a gate
    in the moat on the very road the attacker used; the second found a
    brain re-ordering the same refused build four times a second.

I. INTRODUCTION

    Step 3 left the pillbox silent in bot play: tanks route round a
    gun they know about. The roadmap's answer was 6c: walls to close
    the detours, mines to make the ground itself the defence, roads
    for the squad to repair along, and material to pay for it, so that
    a defender has something to build and the pillbox a road to hold.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0004: pillboxes never fire in bot play (backlog 14);

    section 6's mode table (costs), MIN-01 to 03, 05, 06, 09 to 13,
    TER-01 to 03 in part (walls to rubble, forest felled; regrowth not

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. `rules.rs`: the material, harvest, build, mine and pillbox-
        shell constants, each with its sentence. `map.rs`:
        `Terrain::Wall`, `blocks_fire`, `set`, `tile_centre`,
        `forest_near`; `line_clear` honours walls.
     2. `lib.rs`: `BuildKind` grows to Wall, Road, Mine with cost and
        time; `Mine`; `PillboyTask::Harvest`; `World.mines`;
        `Pillbox.shells`; events Built, Harvested, MineLaid,
        MineExploded, TerrainChanged; `route_for` avoids known hostile
        mines; `approach` for sites the squad cannot stand on;
        validation per kind; `step_pillboys` works by `what.ticks()`
        and `what.cost()`, harvests, lays; a walking squad on a
        hostile mine sets it off; `explode_mine` with the chain;
        tanks moving onto a hostile mine; splash knocks walls to
        rubble and sets off mines; pillbox shells regrow and are drawn
        by the owner's tank; the Observation carries known mines and
        nearby forest.
     3. The patching tool used for these edits applied all-or-nothing
        and twice applied nothing because one block's blank line
        differed; it now applies what matches and reports the rest.
        A lesson about tools, not about the game.
     4. Compile errors: a borrow in `observe` (forest computed before
        `me` moved), two in the client (a colour variable out of
        scope; a match missing Harvest), one in the brain (a V2
        reference). Then 24 tests pass, three of them new.
     5. `fortification_pays`, first run: survived 98 373 vs 94 885
        ticks, alive 6:5, attacker dead 12:12, built 298:0. A thin
        margin. The Fortifier left a gate in the mine ring toward the
        centre, and the attacker's route follows the spoke road, which
        enters the depot from the centre side: through the gate.
     6. The gate removed from the moat (a tank's own mines do not hurt
        it); kept in the wall ring. Second run: 118 231 vs 94 885,
        alive 12:5, attacker dead 16:12, built 288:0.
     7. `--defenders N` on the server; `DEFENDERS=n make debug`. A
        trace of twelve Fortifiers and four RuleBots: 157 mines, 95
        walls, 26 harvests, 7 mines set off, one tank destroyed, and
        1 325 refused orders, 1 310 of them "forest: harvest it first":
        a Fortifier re-ordering a mine onto a forest tile in its ring
        four times a second.
     8. The Fortifier reads its own Rejected events: forest to fell
        first, other refusals to leave alone. First attempt changed
        nothing: refusals land in the tick after the order, and the
        brain read events only on its think ticks, one in five. Moved
        above the rate gate: the proof rose to 124 722 (alive 14:5),
        but the trace still had 1 310 refusals: a Fortifier with full
        material was told to fell the tile, could not (material full),
        and re-ordered the build. Forest tiles now wait until there is
        room for their material; the rest of the ring is built first.
        Refusals in the trace: 54 (40 forest, each once). Proof:
        137 664 vs 94 885, alive 18:5, attacker dead 20:12, built
        431:0.
     9. `make replay` on the default match: 36 of 36. `make lobby-test`:
        PASS, one hash. Documents, this entry, commits.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The proof runs are the lesson of the session. The mechanics all
    worked from the first compile; the doctrine had a hole exactly
    where the enemy came from (the gate on the road), and then a loop
    (a refused order re-issued forever), and only a scripted attacker
    running twenty times showed the first, and a trace's refusal count
    the second. A margin of four percent said "something is wrong"
    where a single trace would have said "the mines went off, fine".
    Each fix widened the margin: 98 373, 118 231, 124 722, 137 664
    against a constant 94 885. The proof as the roadmap wrote it (a
    margin the trace can show, over twenty seeds) is doing the work it
    was meant to.

    Mines as entities rather than terrain was the right call for a
    reason the roadmap did not anticipate: hiding. The map's cells go
    to every client in Welcome and change by public events; a mine in
    a cell would be public. An entity with a visibility rule is hidden
    by the same `observe` that hides a tank in forest.

    Pillboxes still do not fire in bot play. The walls are there now;
    the attackers are not yet made to want what is behind them. That
    is step 6d's defender and attacker doctrine, with backlog item 14.
