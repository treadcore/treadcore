       SUB-CUBES, DAMAGE THAT CHIPS, AND CUBES THAT ROLL OFF

                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    The cubes of 0016 were a fifth of a tile; they are a tenth now,
    the sub-pixels of the first, and a tank is a hundred and sixty of
    them with treads and a turret in the seat's colour on an olive
    hull. The detail is for breaking: damage takes cubes off a model,
    highest first, in a fixed order, and every impact throws loose
    cubes that fly, bounce, roll to a stop and fade. Walls stand three
    levels up on their tile; craters sink two levels with their far
    wall in shadow; both are aligned to the grid. Hidden faces are
    culled, which is what makes a map of a thousand walls draw. The
    frame's boxes got the prior art's bevels. Two findings: solo play
    had never shown a crater, because the window's map was never
    told of one; and the camera "offset" on the big map was the first
    seconds of a 95 000-sprite build, not the camera.

INDEX TERMS

    sub-cube, hidden-face culling, Model::chip, debris, crater, wall,
    bevel, map_keeps_up.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Three asks in one afternoon. First, a review of the frame against
    WinBolo's screenshot, to make them very similar, with the walls and
    craters at different heights. Then, mid-work: more detail, each
    cube of sub-cubes as one would do sub-pixel colouring, so that
    things can be destroyed; and cubes that roll off on collision.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0016: the cubes at a fifth of a tile; the side faces
    local to the model; forest and road as a checker. The frame of
    0015. A prior-art map, constanine, with a thousand walls and
    seventeen hundred rubble tiles, which is where the heights would
    show.

III. METHOD

    Heights first: walls three levels up, craters two down, a pit's
    far wall drawn in shadow; the frame's bevels and a sunken frame
    round the map. A shoot on constanine, which found two things
    (section IV). Then the sub-cubes: the models rewritten at a tenth
    of a tile with hidden-face culling, `Model::chip` for damage, and
    a debris system fed by the events the window already hears. A
    burst shoot to catch the cubes in the air.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. voxel.rs: RISE to 0.8 of a unit; pits for z below ground;
        walls three levels; a crater model. render.rs: `raise_or_sink`
        for Wall and Rubble cells, on the first map and on change.
        hud.rs: the bezel lighter, `sunken()` and `raised()` border
        colours, the map in a sunken frame, the order tiles raised.
     2. Shot on constanine, sixteen seats. The walls stood; the tank
        was nowhere near the map's middle, and the frames taken
        twenty seconds later had it off the screen. Three controlled
        runs: the default map centred; big-lizard centred; constanine
        with eight and sixteen seats centred, once given five seconds.
        The first frames had been taken while 95 000 wall sprites were
        still being built and the camera's lerp had run a frame or
        two. Not a camera fault: a sprite count.
     3. Finding: no crater had ever been drawn in solo play. The
        window's map was cloned from the world at the start and never
        updated; a client's was kept up from events, a host's was not.
        One call to `map_keeps_up` in `step_host`. Finding: the
        newswire said "tank hit -15 (32 left)" for a tank that was not
        mine; it now says whose.
     4. voxel.rs rewritten: `Model { unit, cx, cy, cubes }`; hidden
        top faces (a cube above) and side faces (a cube in front)
        culled; `chip(frac)`; the tank at a tenth of a tile (olive
        hull, treads alternating, superstructure, turret in the seat's
        colour, a barrel of nine), the pillbox as a four-step pyramid
        with the gun on the top step, the depot with corner posts, the
        drones, the wall four by four by three at a quarter, the
        crater four by four two deep, both centred on their tile.
     5. render.rs: `Debris` and `debris`: every impact in
        `View.impacts` throws eight to fourteen cubes at random angles
        and speeds, with a rise, gravity, a bounce at 0.35, drag, spin
        and a fade over the last 0.4 s. session.rs: `push_events`
        fills `impacts` from ShellImpact (earth, or tan if the tile
        was still a wall), MineExploded, TerrainChanged to rubble from
        a wall, PillboxDamaged and Destroyed, TankDamaged and
        Destroyed. The model keys carry damage in tenths (tank) and
        eighths (pillbox) so the cubes are rebuilt as it changes.
     6. Three burst shoots. The first two had no shot: the right
        button's release was aimed inside the gun's minimum range, and
        the refusal had scrolled off the newswire. The third, at
        twenty-three tiles into a wall: tan cubes out of the wall in
        two frames of the burst, the fire lamp dim while reloading,
        the shells at 22 of 24.
     7. Two frames published (17, the models close; 18, the cubes in
        the air, with a 2x crop), the gallery, INTERFACE.md section 8,
        CHANGELOG, INDEX.md; this entry.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The sub-cube is the right size because it is the size of a
    detail: a tread, a gun, a corner post. Below it there is nothing
    to draw and above it nothing to break. The culling is what buys
    it: a wall is the same block it was, drawn with a third of the
    sprites, and the map that stalled the camera does not.

    Destruction in the window and not the sim is the line the design
    has always drawn. The sim knows a pillbox's health and a tile's
    terrain; what a cube does when it comes off is the window's story
    about that, told from the events the sim already sends, and a
    Brain reading the same observation sees no cube. The crater is
    the sim's (a tile became rubble); the earth rolling out of it is
    not, and does not need to be.

    The two findings were the day's real lessons. A window that had
    only ever been run as a client had a host-side gap nobody could
    see until a solo frame was examined for a crater; and an
    apparent camera fault was a performance fact, found by the
    controlled runs, not by reading the camera code again.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The side faces follow the model's rotation, not a light.
    2. The debris does not know about walls: a cube rolls through a
       block it should stop at.
    3. Walls knocked to rubble become pits at once; a half-height
       ruin between would read better, and the sim has no wall health
       to hang it on.
    4. Carried: the text panels' one ink; PLAYABILITY.md items 1, 3,
       4, 5, 6; the window under Wayland proper; two windows; step 7's
       two-router proof; steps 8, 9.
