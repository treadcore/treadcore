                  SOUND: EIGHTEEN CUES OUT OF ARITHMETIC

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 9 is the last on the roadmap: sound, art and a release
    profile. The art and the release profile were already done, by the
    playtest's frame work and the release pipeline, so this session was
    sound. The window synthesises eighteen sounds at start-up (no sound
    files) and plays them through Bevy's audio. Each is placed where
    its event happened, heard from the middle of the view and panned
    left or right, and a hostile shell whistles where it will land the
    first time a snapshot shows it. That whistle is the roadmap's "a
    shell launch you cannot see". The first level check found the
    blasts at a third of the interface tones' strength; filtered noise
    loses power as its cutoff falls. It was fixed before anything was
    heard. Nobody has heard it yet: this machine has a sound card and
    no ear.

INDEX TERMS

    sound, synthesis, spatial audio, levels.


I. INTRODUCTION

    "Sound is the one thing the HUD cannot convey (a shell launch you
    cannot see)." The step's own sentence decided the one cue the window
    derives for itself rather than reading from an event.

II. BACKGROUND

    Journal 0022 left steps 7 and 8 waiting for a second machine. Step
    9's other two parts were in: art in journals 0014 to 0017, the dist
    profile and the per-platform packages in 0018 to 0021. Bevy 0.17
    was built without audio (BUILD.md section 1); ALSA was already in
    make deps, the workflow and the flake. The guest has a virtio sound
    card.

III. METHOD

    1. No sound files. The look is rectangles; the sound is a swept
       sine, filtered noise and a few inharmonic partials per cue.
       Nothing to license, nothing in the tree, and `make sounds`
       writes them out for anyone to hear.
    2. Bevy's audio, with only the WAV decoder: the synthesised samples
       are wrapped as 16-bit WAV in memory and handed over as ordinary
       AudioSources. Rejected: a custom rodio Decodable (a second
       direct dependency on rodio's version), and Bevy's spatial audio
       (its falloff would need tuning in pixel units with nobody to
       listen). Instead, five pre-panned mixes per cue, made on first
       use, and the gain set per voice.
    3. What may be heard is what the sim lets the player know
       (event_visible), plus the whistle: an enemy shell's launch is
       not an event a player receives, but the shell is in the snapshot
       as soon as its flight or its impact point is in view.
    4. The events reach the window before the snapshot they belong to,
       so a sound whose entity is not yet in view (a shell just fired)
       waits up to 300 ms for the next snapshot, then is dropped.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. sound.rs: the synthesis, WAV, placement, the play system; the
        event-to-cue table in session.rs beside the cubes; --mute,
        --write-sounds, M. bevy_audio and wav on; hound was the one
        crate to download. Four tests.
     2. make sounds: the release window built in 82 s; eighteen files.
     3. Measured them, having no ear: a zero-crossing rate for
        brightness, RMS by quarters for the envelope. The booms were
        thin and over in a quarter of their length, at RMS 0.07 against
        0.2 to 0.3 for the interface tones: a refused order would have
        drowned a shell landing. The explosion's noise now keeps its
        power as the cutoff falls; the blasts go through a soft clip;
        each cue has a level (world 1, incoming and launch 0.6,
        interface 0.45). After: blasts 0.16 to 0.17, interface about
        0.09.
     4. A 25-second and a 40-second solo run: no audio error and no
        sound. The trace said why: in the first forty seconds every
        launch belongs to a bot, and a player is told only of its own
        and its allies'. A 60-second run, with a debug line per voice:
        the swarm, three clangs on the player's own tank (centred), a
        gun at gain 0.95 and its boom, a wreck, a refusal.
     5. The docs: HOW-TO-PLAY (a paragraph, the key), the first-minute
        help (one line), BUILD (the feature list), PLAYABILITY item 14,
        ROADMAP step 9, README, CHANGELOG.

V. RESULTS

    source/treadcore-client/src/sound.rs, new; 18 cues, 0.18 to 2.2 s,
    22 050 Hz. make test: everything passes (client 4, new). cargo
    check --workspace: no warnings. The 60-second solo run: 8 voices,
    no error.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The level check is the session's lesson. Every cue passed the tests
    (finite, unclipped, no click, not silent), and the mix was still
    upside down: a test that a sound exists says nothing about how it
    sits beside the others. Measure the thing a listener would notice
    first, which is loudness, before anyone listens.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. A listening session: the levels, the falloff (half at 25 tiles),
       the pan, and whether eighteen is too many or too few.
    2. The first-minute help gained a line, so the release frames
       (make shoot) would differ from the ones in reports/images; the
       next release takes them again, as it does anyway.
    3. The packages now carry a link to ALSA; the workflow's .deb and
       .rpm tests will show it, and they wait on the Actions pause.
