            THE PILLBOYS: ROADMAP STEP 3, THE SQUAD AND THE PILLBOX

                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            12 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Roadmap step 3 was built as DESIGN.md decided it the same
    afternoon: the Pillboys are state on the tank driven by one order,
    `Deploy { task }`, Bolo's builder with the tank as the thing driven.
    The squad walks the world's route at 1.5 tiles a second, captures a
    neutral or dead pillbox by standing on it for ten seconds, repairs
    and builds for material, and walks home; a shell's splash over it
    costs two of four men. Pillboxes are Bolo's with one addition: a
    short-range gun against drones with an interceptor's odds. Twenty
    neutral ones start on the map. A tank's route now keeps out of the
    ring of any live pillbox that would shoot it, because the spoke
    roads run straight through them. The proof asked for was run:
    over twenty seeds of eight bots, the four with squads beat the four
    without 18 wins to 2, 20 945 points to 13 655, 37 survivors to 32,
    with 248 captures. One caveat is recorded: in the default
    sixteen-bot trace no pillbox fired at a tank, because the router
    keeps tanks out; the gun is proven by test, not yet by play.

INDEX TERMS

    builder, squad, pillbox, capture, repair, hitscan, route cost,

I. INTRODUCTION

    Step 3 is the first change to the step function since the replay
    and the hash were built to catch exactly such changes, and the
    first mechanic taken from the WinBolo specification (section 6,
    the builder; section 8, the pillbox). The question was whether the
    decided control model, an order rather than a unit, plays: does
    sending the squad and waiting for it pay, against the same doctrine
    that never sends it?

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0003: the cross-machine replay proof, a replay corpus,
    the window, two machines. The design was written (DESIGN.md,
    "Pillboys and pillboxes: the decision") and checked against Bolo

    roadmap step: BLD-01 to 03, 05, 06, 09, 10, 13; PIL-01 to 05, 07

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. `rules.rs`: the "pillboys and pillboxes" block, every number
        with its sentence. `map.rs`: `pillboxes` (one at 0.36 of each
        spoke, four at 9 tiles from the centre), ground cleared round
        them.
     2. `lib.rs`: `BuildKind`, `PillboyTask`, `Pillboys`, `Pillbox`;
        `Tank.pillboys`; `World.pillboxes`; `Command::Deploy`; eleven
        events; `apply` validation for each task; `step_pillboys`
        (walk, work, home; capture clocks on the pillbox, contested
        means nothing moves); `step_pillboxes` (reload, tanks first
        with a clear line, else the nearest hostile drone, one shot per
        reload); splash damages pillboxes and squads; direct fire hits
        a pillbox when no tank is there; the squad dies with the tank;
        depots restock men and material; pillboxes in the observation.
     3. `cargo check`: one borrow error (route planning while holding
        the tank), fixed by reading first.
     4. `cargo test`: `move_is_routed_across_the_river` failed: the
        tank stopped at (70.3, 64.9), dead. It had driven down the
        spoke through the neutral pillbox at a third of the way and
        the one east of the centre: six or seven shots. The mechanic
        working, and a finding: every start's road to the centre runs
        through a pillbox's ring.
     5. `Map::route_with(a, b, danger)`: a per-tile cost multiplier;
        `World::route_for(player, a, b)` passes 5 inside the ring of
        any live pillbox hostile to the player; `Move` and the blocked
        re-plan use it. The routing test clears the pillboxes; a new
        test asserts a pillbox kills a parked tank in under 20 s and
        that the start-to-centre route stays more than 8 tiles from
        the spoke pillbox.
     6. `pillboys_capture_a_pillbox`: deploy, refused while out,
        captured between 20 and 40 s (13 s walk, 10 s stand), squad
        home at full strength, 25 points, and the pillbox then shoots
        a parked enemy and not its owner.
     7. Proto VERSION 4. Server narrates the eleven events and sums
        pillboxes in the summary. Client: squad line in the HUD, keys
        4/5/6, pillbox sprites with health, range rings (red hostile),
        capture rings, the squad's walk drawn. `describe` gained the
        view so "pillbox hit you" knows whose tank.
     8. RuleBot: doctrine 9 (send the squad from a standing tank with
        no enemy in view to the nearest capturable pillbox within 20
        tiles whose gun cannot reach the tank, or to repair an owned
        one below half; hold while it is out). `without_pillboys` as
        the control. `pillboys_pay`: twenty seeds, eight bots

     9. `cargo test --workspace`: 24 pass, first time.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The proof passed with a margin the trace can show, but the margin
    is not what the roadmap imagined. The squads won by holding more:
    every capture is 25 points, and 248 captures over twenty matches
    are most of the score gap. The survival gap (37 to 32) and the win
    count (18 to 2) are the better evidence that owning pillboxes
    helps, and the trace says how: a pillbox you own shoots swarm
    drones over your approach and is a ring the enemy's router steers
    around, which keeps enemy tanks away from your depot. What the
    pillbox did not do, in any bot match, was shoot a tank. The bots
    now know where the guns are and never drive into them, which is
    correct play and a dull trace. Backlog item 14: the attackers of
    step 6d need a reason to enter a ring, and a defender needs to put
    its pillbox where the road must pass.

    The route-cost change was the session's real design decision. A
    pillbox on the road is Bolo's whole territory game: the gun
    controls the road. With the router steering round rings, the gun
    controls nothing a tank cannot detour; with no avoidance, every bot
    drove through two guns and died in the first minute. The multiplier
    (5x inside a ring) is a middle: a detour of five grass tiles beats
    one tile under the gun, but a ring with no way round is still
    crossed. Walls (6c) will close the detours, and then the pillbox
    will fire.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Pillboxes do not fire in bot play (backlog 14).
    2. Repair and Build are proven only by `apply`'s validation and the
       bot's willingness: no trace shows a repair or a build, because
       nothing damaged a pillbox and the bot has no reason to build yet.
    3. From 0003: the cross-machine replay; the window; two machines.

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. Step 6c: material from forest, walls, roads, mines; then a
       defender has something to build and the pillbox a road to hold.
    2. A scripted scenario in `make debug` (an attacker ordered through
       a ring) so the trace shows the gun.
    3. The window, so the squad's walk can be watched once.
