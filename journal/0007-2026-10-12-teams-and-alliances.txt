                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org

ABSTRACT

    Step 6 gives the world sides. A player has a team; a scenario deals
    the seats into teams or leaves everyone alone, Bolo's start, to
    ally in play by Bolo's protocol: ask, be accepted by any member,
    leave at will, a request standing thirty seconds, all of it events.
    A team shares its picture (an ally's recon spots for your gun), its
    depots, its pillboxes' shells and the knowledge of its mines, and
    is spared friendly fire: an ally's shell, strike, splash or mine
    does nothing and the trace says so; one's own shells still hurt.
    Interceptors and pillboxes leave allies alone; a strike at an ally
    is refused. Team chat. The match ends when one team stands; the
    winning team's top scorer is the winner. The proof the roadmap
    asked for runs as a test: a shell fired at what only an ally's
    drone sees is spotted, and a shell landing on a teammate does no
    damage. A four-team trace of sixteen bots: 61 of 79 shells spotted.
    30 tests pass. Wire version 7.

INDEX TERMS

    team, alliance, request, accept, leave, shared sensors, friendly
    fire, team chat, winning team.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Sixteen players without sides is a brawl; with fixed sides it is a
    bracket. The brief (CONCEPT.md section 9) asked for alliances that
    turn friendly fire off so allies can stampede together, and later
    for betrayal under a three-way rule. Step 6 is the first half: the
    team as a thing in the world, and Bolo's way of forming one.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The team changed one thing everywhere and nothing anywhere else:
    every rule that asked "is this mine?" now asks "is this ours?",
    through one function, and the drone, shell, pillbox and mine
    mechanics are the ones they were. The proof the roadmap wrote in
    the morning (a spotted shell through an ally's eyes; a spared
    teammate) ran as written, once the test stopped killing its own
    recon with its own first shell, which is the rules working.

    The alliance protocol is Bolo's and is deliberately plain. The
    constraint the brief asked for, that sides may change only while
    three remain, is not here: step 6b adds proposals, swaps and the
    three-way rule on top of this, and this step's `Leave` and
    `Request` will be the primitives it builds from.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. No bot forms or breaks an alliance yet; the protocol is proven
       by test, not by play. Step 6b's proposals and 6d's defender
       are where a bot will want an ally.
    2. The window has no key for the alliance commands; the HUD shows
       requests but a human cannot answer one. A small input change
       when the window opens.
    3. From 0006: doctrine distances; tile sprites; the Bolo maps'
       licence; forest regrowth; the cross-machine replay; the window;
       two machines.
