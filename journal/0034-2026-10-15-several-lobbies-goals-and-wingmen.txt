                  SEVERAL LOBBIES, GOALS, AND WINGMEN

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           15 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 8's last item first: one --listen server now sits in several
    lobbies at once, a thread and a UDP port each, and LISTEN=near
    LOBBIES=2 make lobby-test has it host two matches at the same
    time, both replays matching. Then the session turned on a
    playtest remark: a pillbox could not be taken. The numbers allow
    it; the game does not show how. Roadmap step 11 was written
    (commanders, fleets, battle lines, real numbers), and its first
    part built: a Commander brain that drives the player's own seat
    toward a goal (G in the window), and a wingman bot from Paul's
    brainstorm. The hunter takes a live enemy pillbox in 26.9 s with
    two shells and no damage.

INDEX TERMS

    networking, artificial intelligence, game design, testing.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Two questions: what is left of the backlog before a release, and
    why a person cannot take a pillbox that a bot takes every match.

II. BACKGROUND

    A pillbox: 60 health, 8 tiles of reach, 15 every two seconds, at
    tanks and drones only, never at a squad. The direct gun: 10 tiles,
    45. So the ring between 8 and 10 tiles is safe, and two shots kill.
    The squad then holds it for ten seconds. A neutral pillbox may be
    captured live. None of this was written where a player would read
    it.

III. METHOD

    The lobbies: one thread per --game with its own socket, the status
    page on a thread of its own behind a lock, each lobby's files in
    DIR/NAME. One lobby keeps the flat directory and the old
    status.json fields at the top, so every earlier proof reads the
    same.

    The goals: a Brain, not a sim change. The Commander sees what the
    player sees and its orders are a keyboard's, so solo, hosting and
    client all work, and the replay and the wire know nothing of it. A
    RuleBot inside it keeps its doctrine for drones and for tanks in
    sight; the Commander keeps only the orders that serve the goal.

    The wingman follows Paul's brainstorm (a review of a design he had
    drafted): a priority order (protect the leader; what is ahead of
    the leader; the leader's pillbox; a depot by the leader; another
    ally's fight; regroup), a commitment period so it does not dither,
    a leash so it does not wander, alliances as a background layer,
    and defection unlocked, never forced, by holding every pillbox and
    depot. Ownership is read from the observation's full lists, which
    are the world's: territory is public.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. Several lobbies. FAIL on the test's own count: the server's log
       held two hashes and two hosting lines. The script now matches
       the four's hash against one of the server's and the second
       lobby's against the other. PASS. Plain, near, far, introducer,
       introducer with two lobbies, near with a late arrival: PASS.
    2. Step 11 written into ROADMAP.md, 11a to 11g, two of them open
       questions for Paul (real numbers beside or instead; upgrades).
    3. The hunter: FAIL, no shot in 90 s. The tank stopped at 9.80
       tiles and the firing band ended at 9.8: the world ends a route
       at a tile centre. The firing point is now the tile centre in
       the ring nearest 9.1 tiles. PASS, 26.9 s.
    4. A wall on the near side: FAIL, the hunter fired every tick from
       where it stood and gave up after twelve refusals in twelve
       ticks. It now fires only from its chosen point, so a refusal
       moves it round first; a refusal is also read from the shell
       count, which a window's client sees as soon as a host does.
       PASS.
    5. The window: G, the top strip, the wingman seats. One Key Setup
       test had row 29 written in for the last action; it is the last
       row now.
    6. The window driven: one wingman, G once. At 50 s the strip said
       "GOAL hunt pillboxes: the squad is taking it, 7 s", one pillbox
       was ours, and bot1 asked to join on the newswire.

V. RESULTS

    make test: sim 36, brain 17, client 21, net 14, proto 3.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The hard part of taking a pillbox was never the numbers. A goal
    that does it in front of the player teaches it, and the guide now
    says it in five lines (HOW-TO-PLAY section 6).
