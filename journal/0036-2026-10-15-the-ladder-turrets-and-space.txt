        THE LADDER: DEFEND BASE, TURRETS, SPACE, AND FLEETS

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           15 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    The squad can now lift a pillbox of its own side, carry it, and set
    it down elsewhere, as the prior art's builder did (a sim change,
    wire version 14). On that the Commander gains a goal for a base
    under imminent threat and a ladder, Auto, that picks the tank's
    goal itself: defend base, fortify, turrets, hunt, guard, top down,
    each rung switchable. Alone on the island the ladder built the
    castle in 338 s, then lifted both outlying pillboxes and set them
    down in the two turrets by 403 s, and turned to defend the base the
    tick an intruder stood in the gate. Space opens a coordination
    screen that shows the ladder and hands the tank to it; Space again
    is the wheel. --fleets deals sixteen seats into three teams of five
    that build at their leaders' depots, and an aggro bot alone.

INDEX TERMS

    artificial intelligence, decision making, game design.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Paul asked for three things at once: to watch the method play out
    (a GIF, the game from the start, the upper left corner taken), a
    bot for a base under imminent threat, and more options to drive
    "the hierarchy of decision matrix of the new hybrid human bot",
    with an easy way back and forth between driving and coordinating.
    He also described turrets: a position walled in, made by
    collecting pillboxes, once a base is developed and not under
    attack. The world had no way to collect a pillbox.

II. BACKGROUND

    Journal 0035's castle builds new pillboxes for material. Bolo's
    builder could pick up a pillbox and carry it; this sim's squad
    could not. A tank sees TANK_VISION (7) tiles and a pillbox sees
    nothing; walls block a line of fire both ways.

III. METHOD

    1. The sim. PillboyTask::Lift { pillbox } on a pillbox of one's own
       (live or dead): PILLBOX_LIFT_TICKS (5 s) of work, then the
       pillbox leaves the map and Pillboys::carrying holds its health.
       A Build of a pillbox while carrying costs no material, takes
       PILLBOX_PLACE_TICKS (5 s) and sets that health down. A squad or
       tank lost while carrying lets it fall where it was, dead, still
       the side's. Events PillboxLifted, PillboxDropped; refusals
       "only a pillbox of your own can be lifted", "already carrying
       one", both PICNIC. Wire version 14.
    2. Defend base. The base is the castle's centre, else the depot of
       the side nearest the tank when first asked. A hostile tank
       within BASE_ALARM (16) of it is a threat; so is a pillbox of the
       base hit, or firing (the guns are the base's ears, since nobody
       sees past seven tiles). The tank fights under the base's guns a
       few tiles toward the threat, or goes to the pillbox that heard
       it; the squad mends the neediest pillbox between shots.
    3. Turrets. Plan::add_turrets puts a bastion on the wall's ring 12
       degrees past each end of the curtain wall, and one more wall
       tile 14 degrees past that: the pillbox is set into the wall.
       The ends are where an attacker would go round instead of
       through the gate; each bastion is 3.9 tiles from a tower and
       6.2 from the rear pillbox. The Commander drives to the outlying
       pillbox (so the squad's walk is short), lifts it, drives it home
       and sets it down.
    4. The ladder, Goal::Auto. Every tick, top down: defend base (a
       threat), fortify (the castle not standing, and forest to feed
       it), turrets (the castle standing, QUIET 20 s without a threat,
       a slot free and an outlying pillbox of ours, or the turret walls
       to close), hunt (a pillbox not ours, within EXPAND_R 30 of the
       base first), guard. Each rung can be off; each carries a line
       on what its condition sees.
    5. The window. Action::Coordinate on Space opens the coordination
       screen and puts the tank on Auto; Space or Esc closes it and
       gives the wheel back. Stop moves to H. On the screen 1 to 5
       turn rungs, G picks one goal, a click holds there, Tab folds it
       away to see the map. Shift-click in pillbox mode lifts one of
       yours by hand.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. The sim test first: lifted at 40 health, set down at 40 with no
       material, a pillbox of theirs refused, the carried one falling
       dead with the tank. PASS.
    2. make test FAIL: every_refusal_has_a_name. The two new refusals
       had no chair. Both PICNIC.
    3. The ladder, FAIL: it flipped from turrets back to fortify,
       because the turrets' wall tiles counted as castle unbuilt. The
       plan keeps castle_len; the turrets are what comes after.
    4. FAIL: nothing lifted. The tank parked 3.1 tiles from the
       pillbox and the squad was sent only within 3.0, so it waited
       for ever. Within 4.0 now.
    5. FAIL, and a finding: an intruder three tiles outside the gate
       was never answered. Nobody of ours could see it (7 tiles), the
       towers could not shoot it (the wall), and the turrets were out
       of reach. The guns firing now count as an alarm, but none were
       firing. The test's intruder stands in the gate, where the
       castle is meant to meet it; the gap is roadmap 11j.
    6. PASS: fortify to 338 s, turrets from 338 s, both set by 403 s,
       defend base the tick the intruder came.
    7. The window, recorded for Paul's GIF on a copy of the island with
       seat 0 in the north-west. Paul played in it as it recorded: a
       click on the map with the coordination screen up is "hold
       there", so the tank stood and fought where he clicked, and died
       at about 440 s. Before that, fortify had waited seventy seconds
       on "waiting on the site": the window draws one frame for
       several ticks when it is slow (and grim slows it), the pilot
       thought once a frame, and each observation carries only its own
       tick's events, so Built was missed and the plan waited out its
       minute. View::unthought keeps every event for the pilot; and an
       order out two seconds and more when the squad is home again
       counts as done.
    8. The same match headless (the_ladder_in_a_match, by hand) found
       three stalls and a noise: a turret's tile was forest and nothing
       felled it; a wall tile on forest could not be felled with the
       material full, and the plan asked again every few ticks; a
       firing point on water was refused once a second for five
       minutes; and the gun asked for a direct shot through its own
       wall every tick. Each fixed: the slot is felled first, a tile
       that cannot be felled is skipped, a refused Move turns the
       firing point round, and a "no line" refusal holds the gun two
       seconds. Then fortify, a neutral pillbox taken at 290 s and set
       into a turret by 330 s, the second by 450 s.
    9. Fleets. treadcore-brain::fleet deals the seats: three teams of
       five neighbouring starts and seat 15 alone on a RuleBot. A bot
       team: the leader on Auto at its own depot, a guard on defend
       base, two rangers on Auto with fortify and turrets off, a
       wingman. Seat 0's team assists the person: a builder on Auto
       whose base is the person's depot, a guard, two wingmen.
       Commander::base_owner shares the base. The first ten minutes
       headless: two rangers of one team stood on one pillbox for two
       minutes, neither clock moving, because the sim counted two
       allied squads as a contest. Allies now take it together, for
       the one already taking it (allied_squads_do_not_contest). Then
       every team held 3 to 6 pillboxes at its base at ten minutes.
   10. The window recorded again, untouched: no stall this time, the
       castle by 285 s, a pillbox fetched, lifted, carried and set in a
       turret between 320 and 350 s; then shelled from out of sight
       while it sat on the depot, dead at 372 s. Paul's death had the
       same shape. A tank hit with nothing hostile in sight now scoots
       3.5 tiles, alternating sides, sends its recon to where the last
       shell at it came from (a shell carries its origin, and one in
       flight at the tank is in its picture), and fires back there.
   11. Paul: "continue. good ideas and . continue a simulation." Fleet
       matches to the end, headless (16 seats, five seconds a ten
       minutes). With no time limit: every leader dead by 680 s and the
       rest standing guard, or "the leader is not in sight", for thirty
       minutes. And no side on Auto ever took a depot: hunt took
       pillboxes only. Two fixes in the brain, one in the sim: the
       expand rung; a wingman or guard whose leader is down goes to
       Auto at the same base; allied tanks on a depot are not a contest
       (two on their own depot had not even resupplied).
   12. Seven seeds, no time limit: every match decided, 502 to 926 s;
       seat 0's team won five, team 1 two. a_fleet_match_plays_out
       keeps it so.

V. RESULTS

    make test: sim 39, brain 24 (and two by hand), client 22, net 14,
    proto 3.
    a_commander_defends_its_base: five hits on a tank parked twelve
    tiles out, killed, the base's pillbox mended. The GIF of the window
    is in the session's scratchpad; it is not committed.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The ladder is the decision matrix Paul asked for, kept as small as
    it can be: an ordered list of conditions and the goal each wants,
    with the player's hand on every rung. It is still the same brain
    on the same Observation: the window's tank under Auto is never a
    cheat, and its orders are a keyboard's.

    A castle is blind. Walls that stop a shot stop the castle's own
    guns as well, and nothing in it sees past a tank's seven tiles.
    That is a reason for Paul's flag.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Paul's area patterns and pings (roadmap 11i): a rectangle
       cleared and laid out as a line of pillboxes, or a checker of
       road and farm with walls between and pillboxes over the farms,
       declared on the map and queued in a fleet's build list.
    2. Paul's flag on a pedestal, giving a walled town and its allies
       sight once the fort meets its criteria (roadmap 11j).
    3. Allies' goals on the coordination screen: they are not in the
       Observation.
    4. The RuleBot attacking a castle, still unproved.
    5. The swarm: three strike drones took a full-armour tank in 0.2 s
       while it was away hunting. The ladder weighs tanks, not air.
    6. One bot team's leader went hunting with three pillboxes up and
       no wall: the plan starved of forest. A starved plan should ask
       its rangers for material (11e's transfers).
    7. Matches end in eight to sixteen minutes, by elimination. Paul
       asked for longer games; castles slow an attack but no team
       holds one for long, and deaths are for good.
    8. Seat 0's team wins five matches in seven: two wingmen on the
       leader beat two rangers. Fine for a person who wants help; a
       fair fleet start would deal every team the same roles.
