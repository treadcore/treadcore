              BOLO'S COLOURS, AND ASSETS OUT OF CUBES

                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Three questions, three yeses with one condition. The ground is now
    Bolo's palette: cyan water, bright checkered forest, dark grass,
    grey road with dashes, tan walls. Every thing on the map is a
    model of cubes on a grid of fifths of a tile, drawn in an oblique
    projection with sprites, a top face and a shaded side face each,
    so a tank is a hull with tracks, a turret and a barrel, and a
    pillbox a tan pyramid with an orange gun. The condition: the
    window stays two-dimensional. No PBR crates, no tilting camera;
    the cubes stand up by their drawing, as Bolo's pillboxes did. Two
    frames in the gallery.

INDEX TERMS

    palette, voxel, oblique projection, Model, Cube, wall blocks,
    model key.


I. INTRODUCTION

    After the bezel (0015) the map was the last thing not in Bolo's
    look: flat tiles in muted colours, every thing a rectangle in its
    seat's colour. The ask was for Bolo's colours and for assets built
    from cubes, coloured like the pictures.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0015: the text panels' one ink; the air box; the
    playability items. The renderer: one sprite a tile, one sprite an
    entity, placed by id. The workspace's Bevy has sprites, text, UI
    and gizmos and no bevy_pbr, by the decision in BUILD.md that the
    engine build fit an 8 GB machine.

III. METHOD

    The palette first, a function of terrain and tile parity. Then a
    voxel module: a model is a list of cubes; a cube draws as two
    sprites in an oblique projection. Then the renderer: every entity
    a parent with cube children and a label, rebuilt when its model
    key changes; walls as blocks raised when a cell becomes Wall. A
    shoot on a converted community map at three zooms.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. voxel.rs: `Cube`, `Model`, `spawn` (a side face where no cube
        stands in front at that level, a top face lightened by
        height, depth by height and row), and the models: tank,
        pillbox, depot, recon, interceptor, strike, shell, mine,
        squad, wall.
     2. render.rs: `terrain_color(t, x, y)` in Bolo's palette;
        `sync_entities` around models with `model_key` (kind, colour,
        variant); `build_map` raising and dropping wall blocks as
        cells change. One borrow to untangle (the changed cells
        collected, then walked). Built.
     3. The shoot on big-lizard.map, eight seats: the start, the air
        up, two zooms in, one out, a drive. At four wheel clicks the
        tank is a hull of cubes with tracks and a turret; at the map
        scale the pillboxes are tan with an orange top and the depots
        tan plates. Water cyan, forest a bright checker.
     4. Two frames published (15, 16), the gallery, INTERFACE.md
        section 8, CHANGELOG, INDEX.md; this entry.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The oblique cube is the cheapest third dimension there is, and
    the one the prior art used without saying so: a Bolo pillbox is a
    drawing of a pyramid on a flat map, and nobody asked where the
    camera was. Two sprites a cube, no mesh, no light, and the rule
    about rectangles is not bent. A player who zooms in sees a tank;
    a player at the map scale sees a coloured shape with a dark edge,
    which is what the old rectangle was with a little more weight.

    What the cubes do not do: cast on each other, or turn their side
    faces to the light as the hull turns. The side face is local to
    the model, so a tank heading north shows its "front" face toward
    the camera and one heading south shows it away. It reads as the
    hull's front plate either way, and it is not worth a light model.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The side faces follow the model's rotation, not the camera's
       light; see above.
    2. Forest and road checker at one sprite a tile is a pattern, not
       a texture; Bolo's leaf and dash patterns were drawn.
    3. Carried: the text panels' one ink; PLAYABILITY.md items 1, 3,
       4, 5, 6; the window under Wayland proper; two windows; step 7's
       two-router proof; steps 8, 9.
