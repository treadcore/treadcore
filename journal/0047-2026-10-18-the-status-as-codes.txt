                       THE STATUS AS CODES

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           18 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Roadmap 12b's string item, after finding L3: the `why` and `doing`
    strings formatted every think. Measured first, they cost nothing:
    15 us a seat-tick with them or without. The real fault was that
    the brain's logic and the census read the sentences back, so a
    rewording could change play or a count. Every status is now
    written with a code beside its words (status.rs: Act, HopAct,
    CycleAct, KiteStep, Why, and the tactician's Tags), and logic and
    measurement read the codes. A census match is identical before and
    after.

I. METHOD

    The cost: one census match of 300 s, as it was and with the `why`
    formatting stubbed out. The change: a one-off script rewrote each
    status write into a call with its code, 81 in the Commander and 28
    in the hop, after checking that every listed line was a write.
    Five Commander sites that hold a borrow of the fort plan set the
    two fields directly. The proof of equivalence: the same census
    match (600 s, five new switches on) before and after, by stashing
    the change.

II. RESULTS

    Cost: 14.9 and 15.0 us a seat-tick as it was, 15.3 and 15.7
    without `why`, which is noise. Logic that read words, found and
    moved to codes: the guard's busy check, the rotation's "on guard",
    the focus call, and the census's part counts, kiting tally, step
    shots and revive tracking. Equivalence: identical score, kills,
    deaths, thinks, moves and crossings. Brain tests 63 passed, 8
    ignored; client 31; no new clippy warnings.

III. DISCUSSION

    The step was asked for on L3's charge of repeated work, and the
    measurement threw that charge out. It found a better reason in the
    same place: a program that reads its own prose back is one typo
    from changing how it plays. The diagnostic histograms of the quad
    census still read the words, because their value is the detail the
    codes leave out.

IV. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The Observation shared per team: the rest of 12b, a sim change
       that must keep the world hash.
    2. Backlog 18: twelve seeds a switch, longer matches.
    3. Backlog 19: the 70 partial_cmp().unwrap() calls.
