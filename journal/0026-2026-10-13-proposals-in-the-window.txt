                     PROPOSALS IN THE WINDOW

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 6b gave the world proposals (Join, Recruit, Swap, Merge, under
    the three-way rule) on 12 October; only bots could use them. Now P
    opens a list over the map of everyone not on your side. You pick a
    player and the terms, and the list says beforehand what the
    proposal would do and whether the world would refuse it and why.
    Enter proposes and L leaves your side. A proposal to you or your
    side stands on the newswire with its seconds left: Y accepts, N
    declines. The rule's reading is shared: the world's three-way check
    became two public functions, and a test holds the window's verdict
    equal to the world's for every pair and every terms. A RuleBot
    answers a person, so a solo player's proposal is not met with
    silence. Driving the window found a key leaking from a closed chat
    line into the list, and a key hint cut off at 740 px.

INDEX TERMS

    alliances, proposals, user interface.


I. INTRODUCTION

    PLAYABILITY item 7: "the best idea in the design, and the window
    shows a proposal as one roster line and gives no key to answer it."

II. BACKGROUND

    Journal 0025 gave chat. The window showed proposals as "proposal
    #812: p3 Recruit p0". Defenders proposed and answered; RuleBots,
    the default bots, did neither, so a person proposing to one would
    have waited thirty seconds for a lapse.

III. METHOD

    1. One rule, read twice. `proposal_outcome` and `three_way` are
       now free functions in the sim; the world's `outcome` and
       `three_way_check` call them, and `Observation::would_refuse`
       reads the same from the teams every player sees. The test
       `a_window_reads_the_three_way_rule_as_the_world_does` steps a
       clone of the world for all 8 x 8 x 4 proposals in sides of 2, 3
       and 4 and alone, one tank dead, and compares refusals word for
       word.
    2. The list refuses before the world does: Enter on a proposal the
       rule forbids leaves the list open with the reason under it.
    3. RuleBots answer people only. Answering bots would have changed
       bot-only matches with defenders, and the balance proofs rest on
       those traces; a person's proposal is a new case, so it is the
       only one answered: yes, unless the person has hurt the bot (a
       `hurt_by` list from TankDamaged, as the Defender keeps).
    4. Keys: P, Y, N and, in the list, the arrows or WASD, Enter, L,
       Esc. The list takes the keys as a chat line does
       (`View::keys_taken`); its system runs after chat's, so the Enter
       that sends a line does not also propose, and chat does not open
       on Enter while the list is open.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. Sim refactor and the agreement test.
     2. RuleBot `hurt_by` and the answer; `a_rulebot_answers_a_person`
        (accepted; declined after being shot). Brain balance tests
        unchanged.
     3. proposals.rs: targets, answerable, the wording, the list, a
        pure `apply` (three tests); the HUD: the list in the
        scrollback's place, the standing offer on the newswire between
        a refusal and a chat message, readable roster lines, the
        notice tone for a proposal to my side and Capture/Lost tones
        for the answer to mine.
     4. Driven with xdotool. The first run proposed nothing: the list
        had opened and closed before the screenshot. Logging the list's
        keys showed it: in the script, a "p" typed into a chat line and
        the Esc that closed it came in one frame; chat closed the line,
        then the list saw P with no line open and opened, and the
        script's own P closed it again. Chat now marks a frame whose
        keys it had (`keys_eaten`), and the list skips that frame.
     5. The second run, at the shoot's 1200 x 740: P opens the list;
        Merge from a side of three in three is refused beforehand and
        Enter leaves it open; Join to bot1 is proposed, the RuleBot
        accepts, "you joined team 1"; P, L: "you left team 1".
     6. That frame showed the chat session's four-line key hint cut off
        at the bottom: the column clips, and 0025's frame was taller.
        A size down and no gap above; it fits.
     7. The newswire's "you proposes Join to p1" became "you propose
        join to bot1" and "your join to bot1: accepted".

V. RESULTS

    make test passes (sim 25, brain 6, client 15); no warnings.

VI. DISCUSSION

    Twice now a scripted run of the real window found what the tests
    of pure functions could not, both times at the seam between two
    systems in one frame. Both fixes are a line; finding them took the
    window.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Y/N has only been tested in unit tests and through a RuleBot's
       answer, not by a person answering a bot's proposal in the
       window: the only bots that propose are Defenders, which the
       window cannot seat yet.
    2. RuleBots never propose.
    3. The key hint changed again: the release frames differ.

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. PLAYABILITY item 11, the minimap, or 9, rookie bots.
