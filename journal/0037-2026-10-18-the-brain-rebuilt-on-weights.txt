          THE BRAIN ON WEIGHTS: MEMORY, THE BOARD, THE LEDGER

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           18 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    The bot AI was read end to end and written up as a lab report
    (documentation/LAB-REPORT-BRAIN.md, and a shared document with its
    diagrams): a stack of first-match ladders, 22 weights all in combat,
    each tank deciding alone on a full copy of the world twenty times a
    second. The roadmap was rewritten as six goals, the brain's being
    step 12, and step 12 was built the same day: a meter, memory that
    is not recomputed (the castle plan copied once a match instead of
    every tick; crossings answered from a cache 1,368 times against 207
    searches), 56 named weights and 13 capability bits in one flat
    array, smoothing and confirmation, a team board, the base cycle,
    priced crossings, a column on bridges, and a ledger with a tuner.
    Every new part is off by default. The first census could not judge
    them: the baseline against itself differed by five points a seat
    over three seeds.

INDEX TERMS

    utility AI, influence map, Lanchester, hysteresis, evolution
    strategy, Elo, capability flags.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Paul's notes after watching fleets play asked for a lot at once:
    a status report, a logic tree of the brain, research on Terraria
    and Minecraft bots and on RTS AI, a weighted flowchart whose parts
    can be switched off and tuned with sliders, memory and parameters
    first, tanks that give the base something after filling up and road
    out to other bases, small islands treated as poor bases with boats
    preferred to long bridges (six tiles suggested, twenty at most),
    a fleet in file on a bridge, decisions that hold between thinks
    unless running from a shell, a record of each tank's weights beside
    its score, and Total Annihilation's territory, fronts and logistics.
    Then: implement it.

II. BACKGROUND

    The brain on 17 October: 8,458 lines; `make test` last recorded on
    15 October; brain tests 34 passed, 6 ignored (run again at the
    start of this session, 56 s in release). Wire version 16, rc.6 the
    last candidate, Actions paused since 13 October.

III. METHOD

    1. Three helper agents in parallel: one mapped the brain with file
       and line for every claim (checked by hand afterward), one
       gathered the game's status, one read the research and opened
       every source it cites.
    2. The report written into a shared document first, then into
       documentation/LAB-REPORT-BRAIN.md; the old roadmap moved to
       ROADMAP-STEPS-0-11.md word for word, and ROADMAP.md rewritten as
       goals with step 12 under goal 4.
    3. Step 12 built in the order the report gives, each part behind
       its bit, the brain tests run after each so the defaults stay the
       17 October brain.
    4. `brain_census` (new): sixteen seats as `--fleets` deals them, one
       team on the trial settings, the trial team rotated over the
       seeds; naval.map, three seeds, 900 s, respawns 3.

IV. RESULTS

    Tests: brain 48 passed and 6 ignored after the groundwork, 56 with
    the behaviour proofs (the full run is in section VI).

    The census, mean score a seat:

        trial                 trial team   baseline teams
        the baseline itself      13.95         8.93
        every new bit on         10.29        11.53

    Crossing searches 207, answered from the cache 1,368; plan copies
    2 to 5 a match; 28 us a seat-tick. The trial team spent 0 to 44
    seat-seconds a match in the base cycle, 2 to 36 relieving a
    teammate, and laid 4.7 bridge tiles a match against 9.3.

V. DISCUSSION

    The meter's first lesson is the census's own noise: identical
    settings on two teams differed by more than any part moved. A part
    is promoted only by beating the baseline over a fixed census, and
    three seeds is not one. The second is that the cheap wins were
    memory, not cleverness: the ladder's per-tick plan copy and the
    uncached failed crossings cost more than anything the new parts do.

VI. RESULTS OF MAKE TEST, AND THE ANSWERS

    `make test` after the groundwork: sim 50, brain 51 (8 ignored),
    client 27, net 14, proto 3, all passed.

    Paul then answered the report's open questions: every either/or
    is a scalar, how much x and how much y. Each became a weight with
    his answer as its default (ROADMAP 12h): the improvement slot by
    weight (mend, a mine on the approach, works), the road out toward
    bases first, the score between elimination and holding ground, the
    builder for a person's team. His castle: two pillboxes inside, one
    outside on its road, and then no more; the rest line the roads to
    the side's other bases, beside the road and off its junctions
    (`castle_cap`). Alone with three outlying pillboxes and a second
    depot, the capped castle stood with two inside, and the road slot
    and a post toward the second depot were filled by 371 s. Upgrades
    and how to buy them were designed (11g): supply from what a side
    holds, twelve base upgrades and four for the tank, a proposal before
    the sim step. Brain tests after: 55 passed, 8 ignored.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    4. Sliders on the coordination screen; allies' goals from the

    5. The upgrades' proposal (11g), then the sim step; the sliders.
