                     THE REMATCH OVER THE NETWORK

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           14 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    The solo rematch (0029) left the network's to the lobby. Now
    Enter on a networked match-over screen, or after a lost
    connection, takes the window back to the lobby on the same port,
    which looks for peers for twenty seconds so the others meet it as
    they press Enter too. make rematch-test plays it with the window
    and two headless hosts: round 1, Enter, round 2, seated and
    welcomed again. Two flaws the frames showed were fixed: the heading
    said "connection lost" over a finished match, and three of tank
    speak's lines read "you is", "you holds", "you's".

INDEX TERMS

    networking, user interface, testing.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Asked how much backlog was left, the answer was two or three
    sessions, the network rematch the largest piece. Paul also said he
    had played two windows on macOS a while ago, and it was nice:
    PLAYABILITY item 13 records it.

II. BACKGROUND

    The server's --listen loop already runs lobby after lobby on one
    socket (Host::into_socket). A Client had no way to give its socket
    back. The lobby's discover phase is eight seconds.

III. METHOD

    `again` grew a networked arm: the old session is swapped for a
    placeholder lobby with no socket, its socket taken back (Host:
    shutdown and into_socket; Client: leave and the new into_socket),
    and a lobby_session made on it with REMATCH_DISCOVER, twenty
    seconds. The proof wants a host that wins: a headless --net host
    with the smallest peer id, the window with 30 ms of lag, as the
    shoot's network pass has it.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. First proof, against a --listen server: the window was elected,
       not the server. With one seat, that seat's worst path to itself
       is 0 ms and the server takes no seat; the window hosted a
       fifteen-minute match with nobody in it. False start.
    2. Second: two headless --net hosts, one per round, the second
       started at the Enter. Round 1 played; the Enter did nothing.
       The window had been focused once, a minute earlier.
    3. Third, focused just before the Enter: "again: back to the lobby
       for round 2", host2 found, elected, the window welcomed and the
       match played out. The frame showed "connection lost" as the
       heading (the host leaves when its match ends) and "you is the
       last geeker standing".
    4. The match-over heading kept when the host leaves after the end;
       a lost connection mid-match says "Enter for the lobby". Tank
       speak's kill, match-over and intercept lines reworded to read
       with any name; the lingo test renders each with "you".
    5. The rounds-won tally counts networked rounds too, on the first
       snapshot that says a match is over.
    6. scripts/rematch-test.sh, make rematch-test: passed.

V. RESULTS

    make test: sim 36, brain 10, client 21, net 13, proto 3.
    make rematch-test: passed.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The twenty seconds is a guess at how long a person reads a scores
    screen. A group that wants to play again presses Enter within it;
    one who wanders off is not waited for, and the rest elect without
    them. The server (--listen) does the same without anyone pressing
    anything.
