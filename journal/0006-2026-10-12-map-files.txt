        MAP FILES, THE EDITOR THAT IS ANY EDITOR, AND FIVE MAPS
                FROM THE MACINTOSH: ROADMAP STEP 5

                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            12 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 5 makes the map a text file: a format line, `size`, `seed`,
    one glyph per tile, then `start`, `depot` and `pillbox` lines, so
    any text editor is the editor. The loader refuses what it cannot
    use and names the line; the writer and reader round-trip a
    generated map to the bit. A scenario names its map as the
    scenario's seed, another seed, or the text of a file carried
    whole, so replays and Welcomes are self-contained. Mid-session
    Paul produced five prior-art maps (the binary
    format); a converter was written, the maps turned out to be 256 x
    256, and the world was made to take its size from the map after a
    detour through making 256 the default and back. Welcome, now up to
    130 KiB, goes in chunks. Sixteen bots play a converted prior-art map,
    and on it the pillboxes fire: The prior art's roads give the router no way
    round. Rejections on island maps (squads and tanks sent across
    water) fell from 863 to 396 once the bot remembered what
    the world refused. 28 tests pass.

INDEX TERMS

    map file, glyph, round trip, loader, MapSource, the prior art,
    run-length, converter, dynamic size, Chunk.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Pillboxes need placing by hand to be balanced; a seed cannot do
    that. The roadmap put map files here, after the Pillboys and
    before teams, for that reason. The session was meant to be the
    format and its proof. It became, as well, the first contact with
    the game's ancestor's own maps.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0005: forest regrowth, pillbox shells unused by bots,
    pillboxes silent in bot play, the cross-machine replay, the window,
    two machines. The spec's MAP-03 to MAP-06, MAP-09, MAP-10 say what
    a map defines, what a loader repairs, and that a running map can
    be saved.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. `map.rs`: `MapPillbox { pos, owner }`, glyphs, `to_text`,
        `from_text` with `MapError { line, msg }`; tests for the round
        trip and for refusals. `lib.rs`: `MapSource`, `Scenario.map`,
        seats bounded by starts, `map_as_played`. Proto: Welcome
        carries the `Map`; VERSION 6. Server: `--map`, `--save-map`,
        `--write-map`; `make map`; `MAP=file`. Client: `--map`,
        Welcome's map used as is. MAPS.md written.
     2. Paul: look in ~/Downloads/SharedVM for .map files. Five there:
        Constanine, Fido's Airstrip, FortressIsles2, FortressIslesX,
        big-lizard. The binary format, version 1: pills (5 bytes), bases (6),
        starts (3), then run-length rows: a run is datalen, y, startx,
        endx, nibbles; a nibble under 8 means that many plus one
        literal terrains follow, 8 or more means the next nibble
        repeats n - 6 times; a run with no data ends the map; tiles no
        run covers are deep sea. One file ends its runs with 99 99 99
        where the others use ff ff ff; any dataless run is taken as
        the end.
     3. A decoder in Python; the maps' land areas: 168 x 112 to 203 x
        211 of the 256 grid. None fits 128.
     4. Thirteen places assumed 128: swarm edges, the brains' clamps
        and centres, the client's y flip and camera, a test, a log
        line. All made to take the size from the map or the
        observation (`Observation.map_w`, `map_h`, `centre()`,
        `clamp()`); the client hangs the map below the x axis so no
        height is needed. `DepotOwner` on map depots. `Chunk` packets
        and `ChunkAssembler`; the host chunks Welcome; a proto test
        sends a 16 KiB Welcome in pieces, out of order.
     5. The converter, `scripts/priorart2map.py`: terrains mapped
        (building to wall; shot building, crater, rubble, swamp to
        rubble; river, sea, boat to water; mined to plain), owners
        kept, bases as depots with owners said, starts moved to the
        nearest passable tile by ring search.
     6. Paul: "adjust to the map size 256 seems right." MAP_W and
        MAP_H set to 256; the generator made size-relative (forest
        frequency, river bounds, the centre ring). Four tests failed
        for assuming 128 (a shot at the centre from a start is out of
        range at 256; no forest within twenty tiles of a start; the
        proof's squads found nothing within reach). Fixed to be size-
        independent; a doctrine rule added so the RuleBot drives to a
        pillbox worth capturing.
     7. Paul: "i'm wrong about the map size requirement ... if it was
        dynamic sized, then file format of map size should be
        destined size." Default back to 128, with fractions that make
        the generator bit-identical to day one at 128 (30/128,
        98/128). The drive-to-pillbox rule removed: at 128 it sent the
        bots wandering and the proof fell to 3 wins in 20.
     8. The proofs still wrong: `pillboys_pay` 14:6, alive 35:40;
        `fortification_pays` 144 000 vs 75 840. Cause: `Scenario {
        seed, ..default }` now built the *default* map for every seed,
        because `map` defaulted to `Seed(0x7EADC0DE)`. A third variant,
        `MapSource::FromScenarioSeed`, is the default; the proofs
        returned to 18:2 and 137 664 vs 94 885 exactly.
     9. The five maps converted into `maps/prior-art/`; each loads
        (`--map`), 10 to 16 starts, 10 to 16 depots, 5 to 16
        pillboxes. big-lizard, sixteen bots, 90 s: 21 pillbox shots and
        a pillbox kill, the first in bot play; and 863 refused orders,
        791 of them "site unreachable": squads ordered across water.
        FortressIslesX, 60 s: 1 345 refusals, "unreachable" and
        "water", tanks ordered across water four times a second.
    10. The RuleBot reads its Rejected events: a pillbox the squad
        cannot reach and a tile the tank cannot drive to stay refused.
        big-lizard, 90 s: 396 refusals (from 863), the rest fresh scoot
        points the bot computes each time; FortressIslesX, 60 s: 284
        (from 1 345). The proofs unchanged.
    11. `make map` writes `maps/island.map`; `make convert FROM=file`;
        `make replay` on the big-lizard match: all checkpoints; lobby-
        test: PASS, one hash. Documents, vendor notes, this entry.

VI. DISCUSSION

    Three lessons. First, the proofs caught a semantic change nobody
    meant: giving `Scenario` a map source with a fixed default made
    every seeded test play one island, and both twenty-seed proofs
    moved. A default that follows the scenario's seed restored them
    to the digit, which is also the evidence that the generator's new
    size-relative arithmetic is bit-identical at 128.

    Second, the file's size is the world's size. The detour to a 256
    default was short and taught the right shape: constants are for
    the generator; everything that runs takes the size from the map
    it was given. The code that assumed 128 was thirteen lines and
    all of it was wrong in the same way.

    Third, the ancestor's maps make the pillbox fire. On the
    generated island the router steers round every gun; on the prior art's
    islands the road is the only way, and the first pillbox kill in
    bot play happened on big-lizard without any change to the rules.
    Backlog item 14 is still a design question for the generated map,
    but it is answered for the hand-made ones, which is what maps
    were for.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The bots' doctrine is written for 128: a 25-tile standoff and a
       20-tile squad walk are small on 256; island maps refuse half
       their objectives. A doctrine that reads the map's size and
       routes round water (a route-reachability question to the
       world, or a reachable-depots list in the Observation).
    2. The client draws one sprite per tile: 65 536 on a prior-art map, to
       be measured when the window opens.
    3. The licence of the five prior-art maps is not established
       (vendor/THIRD-PARTY.md).
    4. Forest regrowth; the cross-machine replay; the window; two
       machines.
