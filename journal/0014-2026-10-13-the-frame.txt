                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    The window's HUD was rebuilt in the prior art's frame, the same
    afternoon INTERFACE.md drew it: an orders column on the left, the
    map in the middle and never covered, the roster and one lamp per
    pillbox and per depot on the right, the tank's stock as lamps and
    a newswire along the bottom, the lobby and the match's end as
    screens. The pillbox view is the apostrophe, Bolo's key. The gun's
    reach lights while the right button is held. It built first time,
    was photographed in a solo match, a lobby and a twenty-second
    match, and its four frames joined the gallery. A review of what
    stands between this build and a playable game ranks fourteen
    items and costs them; five are a week. Bolo's interface elements
    were checked off one by one against the window before and after.

INDEX TERMS

I. INTRODUCTION

    0013 ended with three findings about the screen (panels that
    overlapped, an overlay that covered the map, a first minute that
    killed the newcomer) and a layout drawn from Bolo's. The
    instruction was to build it, to review what else keeps the game
    from being played, and to look again at Bolo's interface elements.
    The spec's section 13 (UI-01 to UI-23 and the colour code) is the
    prior art's screen written down; this session is its reading.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. Read SPEC section 13 whole: layout UI-01 to UI-07, the colour
        and symbol code, views UI-08 to UI-17, the default keys, the
        message classes. The orders column is UI-02 for this game's
        orders; the lamp strips are UI-03; the lamps for the tank's
        stock are UI-05; the newswire is UI-06 and the message class
        of the same name.
     2. hud.rs: the frame, 330 lines. input.rs: `in_frame` so a click
        on a panel is not an order; `frame_offset`; the pillbox view;
        the gunsight. session.rs: `lobby_peers` with the median rtt,
        `last_tick` for events after a rejoin (0013 finding 5), the
        log at forty. render.rs: the envelope lit from `HudState.
        aiming`, drawn at the predicted position. main.rs:
        `--seconds`. Built first time; the three old warnings.
     3. The shoot: the lobby screen alone; a solo match (the frame,
        air up, aiming, a round out, driving, the scrollback); a
        twenty-second match for the match-over screen. Two rows
        wrapped: the orders column at 104 px and the right column's
        legend at 248. Widened to 132 and 268; the legend on two
        lines; the match-over title deduplicated. Shot again: clean.
     4. Four frames to reports/images (10 to 13) and the gallery; the
        gallery's first figure is now the frame.
     5. PLAYABILITY.md: the verdict, fourteen items ranked and costed,
        Bolo's elements checked off (twenty-two rows), what the frame
        still wants, what the review does not ask for.
     6. INTERFACE.md's "built the same day"; README's controls
        paragraph; CHANGELOG; INDEX.md; this entry.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The frame took an afternoon because the observation already had
    everything the panels show. Not one field was added to it, which
    is the test INTERFACE.md section 5 set: a Brain sees what the
    player sees, still. The work was in where things sit, and Bolo
    had settled that in 1987 for a game with the same shape.

    What the frame changed about play, before any playtest: the map is
    read whole, the tank sits where the eye rests, the shells left are
    a glance, and the gun cannot be fired inside its minimum range by
    accident, because the ring is lit under the cursor before the
    button comes up. What it did not change: a standing tank still
    dies in thirty seconds, which is why item 1 of the review is a
    sixty-second grace in the bots and not anything on the screen.

    The review's shape is the point of writing it: the sim is not on
    the list. Every item is the window's, the brain's or the
    scenario's, and five of them are a week. That is the distance to
    a game a stranger can be handed.
