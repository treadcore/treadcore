          ACROSS THE INTERNET: ROADMAP STEP 7, AND REJOIN

                      Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                            13 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Step 7 takes the lobby past the LAN. An introducer, a UDP service
    that hosts nothing and keeps a registration thirty seconds, tells
    the peers registered under one lobby name each other's public and
    LAN addresses; the lobby greets both until one answers and takes
    the answering one, which is hole punching in its plain form. The
    election and the relay are untouched. Rejoin came with it, because
    identity across processes is the introducer's business: a peer id
    kept in a file, a host that keeps registering, a session number on
    Join, and a seat given back at the tick the match has reached.
    Four peers with no broadcast and no seed address met through the
    introducer alone; a seat killed outright at twenty seconds came
    back to the same seat at tick 371, plain and under 200 ms of lag.
    Two findings on the way were the script killing the host, and the
    predictor checking a tank it had never been shown; a third, older
    than the step, was a crater landing under ticks already predicted.
    40 tests. What is not proven is the roadmap's own proof clause,
    two peers behind two home routers, which needs two machines.

I. INTRODUCTION

    After 0011 the roadmap had two steps left that a single machine
    cannot prove end to end, 7 and 8, and 9 last. The instruction was
    to go on with the roadmap. Step 7 is first in the dependency
    order, its code is all on this side of the router, and the backlog
    had parked rejoin (item 10) on it: "where identity across
    processes is designed once". So the question for the session was
    how much of step 7 one machine can build and prove honestly, and
    whether rejoin falls out of it.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0011: the window unproven in this VM; the predicted
    tank corrected, never smoothed; only the own tank predicted;
    rejoin and the pillbox view where they were. The lobby at the
    start of the session: beacon on the LAN broadcast, `--peer` to
    greet an address by hand, Hello carrying the sender's peer list,
    probe, share, elect. Nothing of it reaches past a home router, and
    a peer behind one has no address it can give.

    The environment is 0011's: Alpine, rustc 1.98.1, four jobs, 8 GB.
    No second machine, no second network, no root.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. Read the backlog (every item done or decided; 10 deferred to
        here), the roadmap, INDEX.md, and the net crate end to end.
     2. treadcore-proto: `Register`, `Introduce`, `Introduced`, and
        `Join { session }`; version 11.
     3. treadcore-net/introducer.rs: `Introducer` (a map from (lobby,
        peer) to two addresses and a time; `poll` reads registrations,
        drops lapses, pushes introductions every second and at once
        for a newcomer), `Registration` (the peer's timer), the LAN
        address by the default-route trick (no packet sent), and
        `identity_from_file`. The test: two lobbies with no beacon and
        no seed, and an introducer, in one thread; both decide on one
        host, each measured the other.
     4. The lobby: `candidates`, `heard`, `targets`, `heard_from`,
        `punch`. A peer named as hosting is pinged, not greeted, and
        joined without an election once it answers; Discover waits
        for it rather than counting it as a peer.
     5. The host: registers every five seconds with `hosting`; answers
        a Ping from a seated peer; on an Introduce naming a seated peer
        that is not joined, pings its addresses (the host's half of
        the punch); on a Join with a new session, gives the seat back
        and starts its sequences over. The client: a session number
        per process. The test `seat_comes_back_after_a_restart`: a
        client dropped mid-match with no Leave, a new one with the
        same peer id welcomed to the same seat 30 ticks later, its
        first order (seq 1 again) applied. 9 net tests.
     6. The server: `--introduce`, `--introducer`, `--no-beacon`,
        `--identity`. The window: the last three. `make introduce`;
        `INTRODUCER`, `BEACON`, `IDENTITY` on `make lan|host|seat`;
        `INTRODUCER=1` and `REJOIN=1` on `make lobby-test`.
     7. First proof run, in the background: `make test` 40 of 40; the
        plain lobby test PASS; `INTRODUCER=1` played a full match and
        then the script died on its own summary (a `grep -c` on a log
        that only exists under REJOIN, under `set -e -o pipefail`);
        `REJOIN=1` killed peer 4, and peer 4 was the host: the three
        seats lost it, the restarted peer found nobody and hosted
        alone with bots. False start, marked: the script now kills
        the highest-numbered seat that is not hosting. `LAG=200
        REJOIN=1`: everything passed except one prediction mismatch
        at 0.000 tiles two ticks after the returning seat's Welcome:
        its copy was the scenario's start, the host's tank had turned
        to fire. The first snapshot is now a baseline, not a check.
     8. Second run: plain PASS, `INTRODUCER=1` PASS (12 introductions
        logged across the four, the host registered as hosting), `LAG=
        200 REJOIN=1` PASS (peer 4 back in seat 0 at tick 371, the
        host gave a seat back once, joined with no election). `REJOIN=
        1` without lag: rejoin itself passed, and a seat that was not
        the returning one logged one mismatch of 0.178 tiles at tick

        under that tank, the Events packet for 604 arrived after the
        client had predicted 605 and 606 over the old map. The class
        of mismatch 0011 said would show up first here. The predictor
        now keeps the last snapshot's tank as an anchor and re-drives
        from it on a terrain event under predicted ticks: the old map
        to the event's tick, the new one after.
     9. Third run, all four: below.
    10. Documents: PROTOCOL.md ("Across the Internet: the introducer",

VI. DISCUSSION

    The step was smaller than its name because the lobby already had
    the shape: it greets addresses, listens, and takes what answers.
    The introducer only supplies addresses the beacon cannot, and the
    punch is the greeting repeated at two of them. The election did
    not change by a line, which was the roadmap's "must not change"
    and is the real argument that a pair of symmetric NATs is not a
    special case: it is a link with no measurement, and that was
    already routed round on the day the matrix was written.

    Rejoin cost one number. The seat was already a peer id's, and a
    peer id was already what the host keyed its table by; what was
    missing was a peer id that outlives a process and a way to tell a
    restart from a repeated Join. The identity file is the first; the
    session number is the second, and the lagged lobby test is why it
    is a number and not "any Join to a joined seat".

    The first run's failures were both the script's and both worth
    keeping in the record. Killing the host was a design error in the
    test, not the code, and it put a line in the roadmap: a host that
    drops is step 8's problem. The 0.000-tile mismatch was the check
    doing its job again, as in 0011: a standing tank, state unequal,
    and the cause found by reading which tick, not by watching.

    The 0.178-tile mismatch is the more interesting one because it was
    there before this session and the previous session's runs did not
    hit it. A crater is a terrain change that arrives as an event,
    reliably but late; the snapshot for the same tick arrives first,
    unreliably but at once. The predictor had already re-driven the
    ticks ahead over the old map when the event changed it. The fix
    is reconcile's tail run again from the snapshot's tank, with the
    event applied at the tick the host applied it. That the host
    changes its map after a tick's drive, not before, is what makes
    "old map up to the event's tick" the right order, and it is the
    kind of fact that a bit-for-bit check forces one to know.

    What this session cannot say: that any of it works across a NAT.
    The addresses on loopback are the same on both sides, the punch
    is a formality, and the LAN address the introducer was given
    (192.168.64.39) was never used. The code paths ran; the routers
    did not.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The roadmap's proof clause for step 7: two peers behind two
       home routers reach Playing. Two machines, two networks.
    2. A host that drops ends the match for everyone. Step 8 (the
       server as a service) is where a standing host lives; a
       re-election mid-match is a design question for then.
    3. A fresh group that picks the lobby name of a running match is
       told its host and refused by it. One name, one match; the
       introducer could carry a match id, and should when step 8
       hosts match after match.
    4. The restarted peer that finds no host (introducer down, host
       gone) waits out Discover and hosts alone with bots, which is
       the lobby's old behaviour and probably not what a returning
       player wants.
    5. Carried: the window unproven here; the predicted tank corrected,
       never smoothed; the pillbox view.
