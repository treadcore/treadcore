                TANK SPEAK, THE SECOND MATCH, AND AN ECONOMY

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org
                           14 October 2026


ABSTRACT

    Three pieces. Tank speak: the players are geekers, and the
    newswire and the bots talk in a cyberpunk, l33t voice with FPV
    pilots' words for the drones and some real tank crews' for the
    rig, picked by the event's own number so no match plays out
    differently for what is said in it. The second match: Enter on a
    solo match-over screen plays again, seen in the window. And bots
    that run an economy: a power plant for a dark zone, the jammer
    once hit, a workshop, scrap felled for all of it; on the Atlantic
    map six structures built in five minutes where 0028's bots built
    jammers only. The balance proofs read the same to the digit.

INDEX TERMS

    user interface, bots, language, testing.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Paul asked for "Geekers" and cyberpunk tank speak, a StarCraft-ish
    l33t, then passed on lists of FPV pilots' slang, the drone war's,
    and tank crews'; then "next on backlog": 0028's next steps were a
    playtest (a person's), bots that build the rest of an economy, and
    PLAYABILITY item 10.

II. BACKGROUND

    From 0028: open problems 1 (fun), 2 (bots build jammers only), 3
    (doctrine on 512 tiles), 4 (rookies against a person). The chair
    (0028's addendum) had set the voice: a tag is a joke at no one's
    expense, recovery is "we".

III. METHOD

    treadcore_sim::lingo holds the words and the banks; callers pick a
    line by a number they already have (a drone's id, a shell's, the
    tick), never by the world's Rng: a bot's chat changes nothing but
    the text of an event. A test holds every line to the voice (no
    "you", no words aimed at people). The economy is a brain change
    only, behind obs.pads, so the generated island cannot see it.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. Asked what Geekers are: the players. Lingo: GEEKER, a lexicon,
       banks for kills, launches, lost flies by why, links lost,
       jamming, intercepts, shots, the end of a match, a bot storming,
       recovery; a quip after each chair tag. The roster's header is
       GEEKERS.
    2. Paul's FPV list: whoop (interceptor), toothpick (recon), send
       it, punch out, WOT, gap, jello, ghosting, failsafed, RTH,
       pancaked, browned out, magic smoke, LQ, VTX, VRX, OSD. The
       drone war's: kami (strike), loitering munition (longshot), the
       kill-zone, the cost-exchange ratio. Tank crews': Power!, bite
       the dust, pigiron, bolo crew (the rookies, and a nod to the
       prior art). Left out: the slurs and the slang aimed at people
       (zipperhead, speed bumps, crunchie, three smoking communists,
       karen, and one more); the test's word list carries them.
    3. HOW-TO-PLAY 6c, the lexicon, generated from lingo::LEXICON.
       Committed (tank speak).
    4. The second match: `again` in session.rs rebuilds the solo
       session on Enter at match over; host_local takes a round that
       reseeds the bots (round 0 is the old seed, so nothing changes
       for a first match); View carries the round and a tally. The
       renderer redraws by comparing cells, so the map's craters go
       with the old match by themselves.
    5. 10j: works_order replaces jammer_order with three wants in
       order; `fell` serves it and mending a wreck. Resupply at a
       working workshop; first version would have sat there for shells
       for ever (a workshop has none) and was caught reading it: a
       workshop is the stop only while armour or the bay is short.
    6. Traces, sixteen bots, five minutes: Atlantic 2 power, 2 jammer,
       2 workshop built; Europe 1 workshop; the industrial island
       none, its numbers as before.
    7. a_rulebot_powers_its_zone_then_builds_a_workshop: a dark
       refinery, a full squad: power first, then a workshop, no jammer,
       the refinery working at the end.
    8. make shoot gains a rematch pass: a 25-second solo match, the
       match-over screen (guide-over.png), Enter, and a check that the
       window logged round 2. The frame after showed "round 2" at tick
       85 with the scores reset. Seventeen frames.

V. RESULTS

    make test: sim 36, brain 9, client 18, net 13, proto 3. Balance:
    16:4, 16950:10720, 35:34, 198 captures, as before.

VI. DISCUSSION

    The industrial island built nothing: its zones lie half way to
    the centre, where a bot is passing, not settled with scrap and no
    enemy in sight. A bot that wanted an economy would have to go to
    its zone on purpose; that is a doctrine change, and a trace's.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. Whether any of it is fun (from 0027).
    2. Bots build only when settled at a zone; on the industrial
       island, never.
    3. The bots' doctrine on 512 tiles (from 0027).
    4. Whether a person finds a rookie beatable (from 0028).
    5. The rematch across a network (step 8).

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. A playtest: ROOKIES=3 make run, three rounds with Enter.
    2. A bot that goes to its zone to build when it has scrap.
    3. PLAYABILITY item 6's rest: chat to nearby and chosen players.
