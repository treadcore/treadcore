               EVERY PART ON, AND A DODGE AGAINST EVERY SHELL

                     Paul Richeson, treadcore.org

ABSTRACT

    The rule of goal 4 changed: a part lands on, with starting numbers,
    and the census guards that the brain still works. The planned dodge
    went on (LR 54); then the eleven parts built and off (LR 55): -0.85
    +/- 0.59 a seat against them off, inside the noise, about 30% more
    brain time. The dodge then planned against every shell in flight
    (LR 56): two shells 76.5% to 100%, four 56.6% to 99.7%.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

    1. `dodge_plan` and `dodge_release` default 1; tests green;
       committed and pushed (61c368b).
    2. Every Caps bit on. Two tests read `Caps::BASELINE` as "off";
       their off cases now name the part.
    3. The guard census, every part on against LR 54's defaults, 48
       seeds: -0.85 +/- 0.59; 38 us a seat-tick.
    4. Two-shell families: 76.5% and 79.9% with LR 53's plan.
       `drive_path`, `Dodge::threats`, `Dodge::clearance`; `dodge_every`:
       100% and 100%; three shells 100%, four 99.7%.

VI. DISCUSSION

    Eighteen parts were built off since LR 11; three were promoted by
    census, because 48 seeds resolve about a point a seat and most
    parts move less. Turned on together they cost less than a point,
    and the brain plays every part it has. The census now guards and
    the tuner will set the numbers.

VIII. NEXT STEPS
