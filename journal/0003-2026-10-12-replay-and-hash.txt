ABSTRACT

    Roadmap steps 1 and 2 were built together, because the hash is
    what makes a replay checkable. `World::hash()` is FNV-1a over the
    bincode serialisation of the world, so every field is in the digest
    the day it is added and floats hash by their bits. `Replay` records
    the scenario, every tick's orders and the hash every hundred ticks;
    `Replay::verify()` replays and names the first checkpoint that
    differs. The server records (`--record`) and plays back (`--replay`,
    exit 1 on a mismatch); `make debug` now writes a replay beside its
    trace and `make replay` checks the newest. The host hashes every
    thousand ticks and carries the hash in heartbeats; clients log it;
    `make lobby-test` asserts one hash at tick 1 000 in all four logs.
    A recorded sixteen-bot match (3 600 ticks, 535 orders, 42.9 KB)
    replayed to all 36 checkpoints in 0.2 s; the same file with the
    indirect shells removed failed at tick 100. The wire is version 3.

INDEX TERMS

    replay, world hash, FNV-1a, bincode, checkpoint, desync detection,
    regression test, heartbeat.


I. INTRODUCTION

    Every later step (prediction, Pillboys, alliances) changes the step
    function. The roadmap wanted, before any of them, a way to say that
    a change did only what it meant to: a corpus of recorded matches
    that still replay to the same hashes. This session built that
    instrument.

II. BACKGROUND

    Carried from 0002: step 0 closed except the window (no Vulkan
    driver here), the release engine (built since: 444 s, 4 jobs, no
    swap), and two machines. The sim already had the property the
    replay needs: the same seed and the same orders give the same
    world (`deterministic` test); it lacked a digest to say so cheaply
    and a record of the orders to say it again later.

IV. CHRONOLOGY

     1. `treadcore-sim`: bincode added as a dependency; `World::hash`,
        `fnv1a64`, `World::run`; `Replay` with `record`, `orders_for`
        (partition_point over the tick-sorted orders), `final_hash`,
        `verify`; `CHECKPOINT_EVERY` 100. Two tests: `hash_follows_
        state` (two worlds agree at every tick; one extra order
        disagrees) and `replay_reproduces_the_world` (record 600 ticks,
        replay, serde round trip, tamper and catch).
     2. `treadcore-proto`: `Heartbeat { peer, tick, hash_tick, hash }`;
        VERSION 3.
     3. `treadcore-net`: `Host.replay`, `Host::record`, `hash_mark`;
        the host hashes at every `HASH_EVERY` (1 000) tick and at the
        end, logs "world hash at tick N", carries it in heartbeats; the
        client stores `host_hash` and logs it when it changes.
     4. `treadcore-server`: `--record FILE` (local and hosted),
        `--replay FILE` (narrates the replay like a live match, checks
        every checkpoint, exit 1 on a mismatch). The client HUD shows
        the host's hash.
     5. Scripts: `make debug` records `build/logs/replay-STAMP.json`;
        `scripts/replay.sh` and `make replay [REPLAY=file]`;
        `lobby-test.sh` asserts the hash at tick 1 000.
     6. `cargo test --workspace`: 19 of 20 pass; `replay_reproduces_
        the_world` failed on its checkpoint count (V.B).
     7. `make debug`: sixteen bots, 180 game seconds, replay written:
        42 902 bytes, 535 orders, 36 checkpoints.
     8. `make replay`: every checkpoint matched (36) in 0.2 s wall.
     9. The replay with every FireIndirect order removed (535 -> 483

    B. The test that failed

      `replay_reproduces_the_world` recorded 170 ticks, not 600: its
      scenario said `swarm_interval: 10`, ten ticks, and two swarm
      drones every half second killed three of four tanks by tick 170,
      which ended the match. `step` on a finished world is a no-op and
      the recording stopped with it, correctly. The test now swarms
      every ten seconds, and `Replay::record` ignores orders offered
      to a finished world, since they were not applied. A test that
      fails for the right reason on the first run is a test that
      works.

VI. DISCUSSION

    A replay is cheap and the hash is cheap: serialising a sixteen-tank
    world is a few tens of kilobytes, and FNV-1a over it is nothing
    next to the step. Hashing every tick on the host would still be
    affordable; it is done every thousand ticks because nothing reads
    it faster yet. When the client predicts (step 4) it will want the
    hash in every snapshot, and the snapshot already carries the
    observation; that is the time to move it.

    The tamper test is the point of the checkpoints. A final hash says
    "something changed"; the checkpoint says "by tick 100", and the
    narrated replay between the last good checkpoint and the first bad
    one is the story of what. That is the debugging tool the roadmap
    asked for, and it costs one line of JSON every five seconds.

VII. OPEN PROBLEMS

    1. The cross-machine proof: a replay recorded here, verified on
       another machine (the host machine, or a CI runner). Expected to
       pass: bincode is byte-exact and Rust's f32 arithmetic has no
       fused multiply-add by default; but expected is not measured.
    2. A replay corpus: a directory of recorded matches checked in or
       published, and `make replay-all`. `RELEASE.md` should make it a
       release gate.
    3. The window and two machines, from 0002.

VIII. NEXT STEPS

    1. Record three or four reference matches (seeds, player counts)

    2. Roadmap step 3, Pillboys, now that a change to `step` can be
